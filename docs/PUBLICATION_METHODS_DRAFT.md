# Methods draft: thickness-aware TCAD model of ultrathin W-doped In2O3 transistors

Draft text for a Methods/Supplementary section. Numbers in square brackets are read from the package tables at submission time (tables/MATERIAL_PARAMETERS_USED.csv, tables/EXPERIMENTAL_VS_SIMULATION.csv); statements about what was NOT done are part of the text and must not be removed.

## Device simulation

Two-dimensional drift-diffusion simulations were performed with Silvaco ATLAS (version 5.28.1.R, DeckBuild 5.0.10.R) using a direct structural description of the bottom-gate, top-contact transistors: a 15 nm HfO2 / 2 nm Al2O3 gate stack (relative permittivities 19.57 and 9.0, from the C-V of the same process [Januar 2026, SI]) on an ideal metal gate representing the TiN electrode, an IWO channel of thickness t = 2.0, 6.3, 13.2 or 31.8 nm (X-ray reflectivity thicknesses of the measured films), and 2 um-wide source/drain contacts at the channel ends with a 20 um channel length. The simulated width is 1 um so that the terminal currents are numerically in A/um; the measured currents (W = 290 um) were converted to A/um once and never divided a second time. No process (ATHENA) simulation was used: the amorphous IWO chemistry is not predicted by a process simulator, and all material parameters were specified directly.

The IWO channel is described by a single material model M(t) whose thickness dependence is carried by explicit laws rather than by per-device parameter sets. Quantum confinement is included as a thickness-dependent band gap and electron affinity, Eg(t) = Eg,bulk + dEg(t) and chi(t) = chi,bulk - dEg(t), with dEg(t) = 0.921 t^-1.382 eV fitted to the slab-minus-bulk PBE band-gap shifts of pure In2O3 slabs (1.98 and 0.95 nm [Lin 2022], 1.5 nm [Si 2021]); the whole shift is assigned to the conduction band, as found in the same calculations. The conduction-band effective mass follows m*(t) = 0.208 + 0.131 t^-1.412 m0 (bulk value from optical Hall measurements [Stokey 2021], increment from the slab calculations), and the effective density of states Nc(t) = 2.51e19 (m*/m0)^1.5 cm^-3 is derived from it. Because Nc is thickness-corrected explicitly, no additional quantum-correction (BQP or Schrodinger) model was activated, avoiding a double count. The static permittivity of the IWO film, 9.30, is the C-V value of the same process. The DFT inputs are pure-In2O3 proxies: no DFT of W-doped In2O3 slabs was executed for this work (the Quantum ESPRESSO inputs are supplied), and PBE absolute gaps were never used, only slab-minus-bulk differences.

Sub-gap states were represented by the ATLAS continuous density-of-states model: an acceptor-like conduction-band tail g(E) = NTA exp[(E - Ec)/WTA] with a shared characteristic energy WTA = 0.040 eV and an integrated density Nt(t) = 2.0e19 (2 nm / t)^0.75 cm^-3 (anchored to the real-solver calibrations of two independent lineages and to the ~4x increase reported between 13.2 and 2 nm [Januar 2026]); a small deep acceptor Gaussian (5e16 cm^-3 eV^-1, 0.6 eV below Ec, 0.15 eV wide, assumed); and an acceptor-like interface-state distribution of 3e11 cm^-2 eV^-1 (Gaussian, centred 0.3 eV below Ec, 0.12 eV wide), implemented as a volume density in a 0.25 nm regularization layer adjacent to the Al2O3 interface and moment-matched to the sheet density (refinement to 0.125 nm changed the current by < 0.01 decade). Interface states and fixed charge are distinct: the fixed charge Qf at the IWO/Al2O3 interface is a single value shared by all thicknesses and is the only fitted electrostatic quantity; it absorbs the unknown absolute alignment of chi,bulk, the TiN work function (4.70 eV assumed) and any true fixed charge, which one transfer curve per device cannot separate. The effective background donor density follows Nd(t) = 2.5e17 [1 + (t/20 nm)^4] cm^-3 and is an electrically active density three to four orders of magnitude below the W atomic concentration; it is not derived from the W content. The equilibrium free-electron density is a solver output.

Electron transport used a constant band mobility per thickness multiplied by a surface-roughness factor (1 - Dsr/t)^2 with Dsr = 0.287 nm (AFM roughness of the same films [Januar 2026]); the band mobility values are fitted (18.1 and 61.9 cm^2 V^-1 s^-1 for the 2.0 and 13.2 nm devices; 12.4 cm^2 V^-1 s^-1, the value of the earlier per-device calibration, was used unchanged for the 6.3 nm validation). The gate-voltage dependence of the effective mobility arises in the simulation from the free/trapped partition of the induced charge in the tail states, not from an empirical mobility-degradation model; the multiple-trapping form used in the analytical model of the paper was not imposed, because the installed solver ignores mobility statements between bias points and its field-dependent surface-mobility models are silicon calibrations. Source/drain contacts were Ohmic (Pd volumes without an imposed work function; no transfer-length data were available). Fermi-Dirac statistics, Shockley-Read-Hall recombination and 300 K were used with an electrons-only solution (hole currents were verified to be < 1e-38 A). The simulated structure contained the physical volumes of the stack (a 50 nm conductor volume for the TiN gate with a 4.70 eV work function, the Al2O3 and HfO2 layers with the measured permittivities, 70 nm Pd source/drain volumes and air above the channel); it was verified to give the same currents (within 0.1 %) as a reduced deck with ideal metal boundaries.

Numerical settings: Newton solution with a strict acceptance criterion (both update and residual norms, absolute current tolerance 1e-17 A/um) in depletion, where the default residual tolerance was found to accept unconverged points at the 1e-15 A/um level, and the default criterion in the on state; 96/48 acceptor/donor energy levels for the density-of-states integration; 0.25 nm vertical spacing in the channel graded to 0.0625 nm at the gate interface; stepped gate sweeps at VD = 0.7 V (0.2 V steps from -3 to -1 V, 0.05 V from -1 to 1.5 V, 0.1 V to 3 V), which were verified to give the same currents as 0.05 V point-by-point sweeps. Doubling the lateral mesh density, the number of energy levels, or halving the regularization layer changed the drain current by at most 0.01 decade (1.1 % at VG = 3 V for the energy levels). Kirchhoff consistency of the terminal currents was verified at every bias point (|ID + IS + IG| <= 1e-16 A). Below ~1e-17 A/um the solver returns zero current; these points were reported as zeros and were neither replaced by a floor nor compared with the measured off-state plateaus, whose origin (5e-15 to 7e-12 A/um) is not identified from the available data.

## Calibration and metrics

Calibration proceeded in stages with the number of free parameters stated at each stage: (1) the confinement, effective-mass and density-of-states laws were fixed from the literature inputs above; (2) the trap laws were fixed from the anchors above; (3) the shared fixed charge Qf was fitted to the constant-current threshold voltages of the 2.0 and 13.2 nm devices (one value, 1.73e12 cm^-2, i.e. a -0.31 V flat-band offset); (4) the band mobility of each of these two devices was fitted to its on-state current; (5) the 6.3 nm device was then simulated with all parameters unchanged as an out-of-sample test. Free parameters: one shared electrostatic value and two band mobilities for the two calibrated devices, against four to six tuned parameters per device in the earlier per-device calibrations. The 31.8 nm device, outside the thickness series of the analytical model and always-on, was not included.

Threshold voltage (constant current, 1e-9 A/um), subthreshold swing (steepest 0.2 V window above five times the measured off-state floor of the same device, applied identically to the simulation), field-effect mobility gm,max L/(W Cox VD) with Cox = 8.96e-7 F/cm^2, on-current at VG = 3 V and minimum current were extracted from measured and simulated curves with the same script. Agreement is quantified by the root-mean-square log10 residual over the active region (currents above five times the measured floor): 0.037 and 0.081 decades for the 2.0 and 13.2 nm devices (on-current within 0.1 %, threshold within 30 mV, subthreshold swing within 15 %). For the 6.3 nm device the untuned model reproduces the subthreshold swing (108 vs 115 mV/dec) but predicts the threshold 0.29 V too negative and, consequently, the on-current 27 % too high; a single device-specific flat-band offset removes the discrepancy, whose origin could not be determined from the available data. The model is calibrated on the same transfer curves it reproduces; no independent bias, temperature or capacitance data were available for validation, and the results should be read as a self-consistent, thickness-resolved parameter extraction with one documented out-of-sample failure rather than as a predictive model of thickness scaling.
