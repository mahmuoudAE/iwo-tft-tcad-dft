\chapter{Electronic-structure theory and its numerical realisation}
\label{ch:mono-dft-theory}

\Cref{sec:dft-theory} introduced density-functional theory in plain words. This chapter gives the derivations behind
every step that the first-principles results of this monograph depend on, and it states for each step which
approximation it introduces and how large its effect is in the calculations of this project. The aim is that a reader
can trace any computed number in \cref{ch:dft,ch:mono-masses,ch:mono-w-vac,ch:mono-amorph} back through a chain of
stated approximations, each with an estimate of its size. Standard results follow \citet{Martin2004} and
\citet{Payne1992}; where a derivation is checked against a number logged by the project, the fact identifier is given.

% =====================================================================================================================
\section{The many-body problem and the adiabatic separation}
\label{sec:mono-dft-bo}

For $N$ electrons at $\vr_i$ and nuclei of charge $Z_I e$ and mass $M_I$ at $\vR_I$, the non-relativistic Hamiltonian
in Hartree atomic units ($\hbar = m_e = e = 4\pi\varepsilon_0 = 1$) is
\begin{equation}
\Hop = -\sum_i \tfrac12\nabla_i^2 - \sum_I \frac{1}{2M_I}\nabla_I^2
 + \tfrac12\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{1}{|\vr_i-\vr_j|} - \sum_{i,I}\frac{Z_I}{|\vr_i-\vR_I|}
 + \tfrac12\sum_{I\neq J}\frac{Z_IZ_J}{|\vR_I-\vR_J|} .
\label{eq:mono-dft-H}
\end{equation}
Writing the full wave function as $\Psi(\{\vr\},\{\vR\}) = \sum_k \chi_k(\{\vR\})\,\Phi_k(\{\vr\};\{\vR\})$, where
$\Phi_k$ are eigenstates of the electronic Hamiltonian $\Hop_e$ (all terms of \cref{eq:mono-dft-H} except the nuclear
kinetic energy) at fixed nuclei with energies $E_k(\{\vR\})$, and projecting the Schr\"odinger equation on $\Phi_k$
gives
\begin{equation}
\Big[-\sum_I\frac{\nabla_I^2}{2M_I} + E_k(\{\vR\})\Big]\chi_k
 - \sum_{k'}\sum_I \frac{1}{2M_I}\Big(2\langle\Phi_k|\nabla_I\Phi_{k'}\rangle\cdot\nabla_I
 + \langle\Phi_k|\nabla_I^2\Phi_{k'}\rangle\Big)\chi_{k'} = \mathcal{E}\chi_k .
\label{eq:mono-dft-bo-coupled}
\end{equation}
The Born--Oppenheimer approximation \citep{Born1927} drops the non-adiabatic couplings in the second sum. Born and
Oppenheimer organised the corrections in powers of $\kappa = (m_e/M_I)^{1/4}$, which is 0.076 for O and 0.047 for In:
vibrational energies appear at order $\kappa^2$ and the non-adiabatic terms at higher order. The nuclei then move on the potential-energy surface $E_0(\{\vR\})$, which is what a
relaxation minimises. The approximation fails where electronic states cross as the nuclei move; for the closed-shell
oxide cells of this project no such crossing is involved in the quantities used, but polaron formation
(\cref{sec:mono-defect-polaron}) is an example where the coupling of a localised electron to the lattice has to be
treated explicitly.

\section{Density-functional theory}
\label{sec:mono-dft-hk}

\paragraph{Hohenberg--Kohn theorems.} Let $v(\vr)$ be the external potential of the nuclei and $n(\vr)$ the
ground-state density. (i) $v$ is determined by $n$ up to a constant. Proof by contradiction \citep{Hohenberg1964}:
suppose $v_1 \neq v_2 + \mathrm{const}$ have non-degenerate ground states $\Psi_1 \neq \Psi_2$ with the same density
$n$. The variational principle gives $E_1 < \langle\Psi_2|\Hop_1|\Psi_2\rangle = E_2 + \int (v_1-v_2)\,n\,d\vr$ and, by
symmetry, $E_2 < E_1 + \int (v_2-v_1)\,n\,d\vr$. Adding the two gives $E_1+E_2 < E_1+E_2$, a contradiction. (ii) The
functional $E_v[n] = F[n] + \int v\,n\,d\vr$, with the universal functional $F[n] = \min_{\Psi\to n}\langle\Psi|\hat T +
\hat V_{ee}|\Psi\rangle$ (constrained search; this form avoids the $v$-representability problem of the original
proof), is minimised by the ground-state density, and its minimum is the ground-state energy.

\paragraph{Kohn--Sham construction.} \citet{Kohn1965} replace the interacting system by non-interacting electrons in
orbitals $\psi_j$ with occupations $f_j$ and the same density, $n = \sum_j f_j|\psi_j|^2$, and write
\begin{equation}
E[n] = T_s[\{\psi\}] + \int v\,n\,d\vr + \tfrac12\iint \frac{n(\vr)n(\vr')}{|\vr-\vr'|}\,d\vr\,d\vr' + \Exc[n] + E_{II},
\label{eq:mono-dft-EKS}
\end{equation}
where $T_s$ is the non-interacting kinetic energy and $\Exc$ collects everything else: exchange, correlation and the
difference $T - T_s$. Minimising \cref{eq:mono-dft-EKS} with respect to $\psi_j^*$ subject to orthonormality,
$\delta\{E - \sum_{jk}\Lambda_{jk}(\langle\psi_j|\psi_k\rangle - \delta_{jk})\}=0$, and diagonalising the Hermitian
multiplier matrix $\Lambda$ by a unitary rotation of the occupied orbitals, gives the Kohn--Sham equations
\begin{equation}
\Big[-\tfrac12\nabla^2 + \vext(\vr) + v_{\mathrm{H}}[n](\vr) + \vxc[n](\vr)\Big]\psi_j = \varepsilon_j\psi_j ,\qquad
v_{\mathrm{H}} = \int\frac{n(\vr')}{|\vr-\vr'|}d\vr' ,\quad \vxc = \frac{\delta\Exc}{\delta n} .
\label{eq:mono-dft-KS}
\end{equation}
Summing the eigenvalues double-counts the Hartree energy and replaces $\Exc$ by $\int\vxc n$; the total energy is
therefore
\begin{equation}
E = \sum_j f_j\varepsilon_j - \tfrac12\iint\frac{n(\vr)n(\vr')}{|\vr-\vr'|} + \Exc[n] - \int\vxc\, n\,d\vr + E_{II} .
\label{eq:mono-dft-Edc}
\end{equation}
Only $\Exc$ is unknown; every approximation of a DFT calculation, apart from numerical ones, is an approximation of it.

\paragraph{Meaning of the eigenvalues.} The Kohn--Sham eigenvalues are Lagrange multipliers, not quasiparticle
energies. Janak's theorem \citep{Janak1978}, $\partial E/\partial f_j = \varepsilon_j$, and the ionisation-potential
theorem for the exact functional, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{HOMO}} = -I$, give the highest occupied eigenvalue a physical
meaning; the others, in particular the unoccupied ones, have none in principle. Band structures, gaps, masses and
level positions read from Kohn--Sham eigenvalues are therefore approximations whose quality has to be checked, which is
what the hybrid-functional checks of this project do (\cref{sec:dft-hse,sec:mono-dft-hybrid}).

\paragraph{Finite temperature and fractional occupations.} For partly filled bands the variational quantity is the
Mermin free energy \citep{Mermin1965}, $F = E - TS$ with the entropy of the occupations; this is the formal basis of
the smearing methods of \cref{sec:mono-dft-smearing}.

\section{Exchange--correlation functionals and the band-gap problem}
\label{sec:mono-dft-xc}

\paragraph{Semi-local functionals.} The local-density approximation takes $\Exc = \int n\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{xc}}^{\mathrm{hom}}(n)$
from the homogeneous electron gas. The generalised-gradient approximation adds the reduced gradient $s =
|\nabla n|/[2(3\pi^2)^{1/3}n^{4/3}]$; PBE \citep{Perdew1996} writes the exchange energy as $E_x = \int n\,
\epsilon_x^{\mathrm{hom}}(n)F_x(s)$ with the enhancement factor
\begin{equation}
F_x(s) = 1 + \kappa - \frac{\kappa}{1+\mu s^2/\kappa}, \qquad \kappa = 0.804,\ \mu = 0.21951 ,
\label{eq:mono-dft-pbe}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa$ enforces the Lieb--Oxford bound and $\mu$ recovers the linear response of the uniform gas. PBE
contains no fitted parameters; its constants follow from exact constraints. It is used for every relaxation and every
production number of this project.

\paragraph{Self-interaction and delocalisation.} In \cref{eq:mono-dft-EKS} each electron interacts with the Hartree
potential of the whole density, including itself. For the exact functional $\Exc$ cancels this self-interaction; for
PBE the cancellation is incomplete, so the energy is a convex rather than a piecewise-linear function of fractional
electron number. A convex $E(N)$ favours spreading charge, so PBE tends to delocalise electrons that should localise
(small polarons, deep defect states, d states of transition-metal impurities). For this project the consequence is
directional: a localised state found in PBE is strong evidence; an extended state found in PBE is weaker evidence
(\cref{sec:mono-am-scope}).

\paragraph{The band-gap problem.} The fundamental gap is $E_g = I - A$ with $I = E(N-1) - E(N)$ and $A = E(N) -
E(N+1)$. For the exact functional, $I = -\varepsilon_{\mathrm{HOMO}}(N)$ and $A = -\varepsilon_{\mathrm{HOMO}}(N+1)$.
Because the exact $\vxc$ jumps by a constant $\Delta_{\mathrm{xc}}$ when the electron number crosses an integer
\citep{PerdewLevy1983,ShamSchluter1983}, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{HOMO}}(N+1) = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{LUMO}}(N) +
\Delta_{\mathrm{xc}}$, so
\begin{equation}
E_g = \underbrace{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{LUMO}}(N) - \varepsilon_{\mathrm{HOMO}}(N)}_{E_g^{\mathrm{KS}}} + \Delta_{\mathrm{xc}} .
\label{eq:mono-dft-gap}
\end{equation}
Semi-local functionals have $\Delta_{\mathrm{xc}} = 0$ and, in addition, approximate eigenvalues, so they underestimate
gaps. For \InO{} the computed PBE gap is 0.8887~eV at the PBE lattice \fact{002}, against an experimental fundamental
gap of about 2.7--2.9~eV (dipole-forbidden at $\Gamma$; strong absorption sets in several tenths of an eV higher)
\citep{Walsh2008,Irmscher2014}. For this reason no absolute gap is taken from PBE anywhere in the project; only
differences between films and bulk (confinement shifts) are used, and their transfer from PBE is checked with a hybrid
functional.

\section{Hybrid functionals}
\label{sec:mono-dft-hybrid}

Mixing a fraction $\alpha$ of non-local Hartree--Fock exchange into the functional restores part of the derivative
discontinuity and reduces the self-interaction error. HSE \citep{Heyd2003,Heyd2006,Krukau2006} splits the Coulomb
kernel with the error function,
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{r} = \underbrace{\frac{\mathrm{erfc}(\omega r)}{r}}_{\mathrm{SR}} + \underbrace{\frac{\mathrm{erf}(\omega r)}{r}}_{\mathrm{LR}},\qquad
\Exc^{\mathrm{HSE}} = \alpha E_x^{\mathrm{HF,SR}}(\omega) + (1-\alpha)E_x^{\mathrm{PBE,SR}}(\omega) + E_x^{\mathrm{PBE,LR}}(\omega) + E_c^{\mathrm{PBE}},
\label{eq:mono-dft-hse}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 1/4$ and $\omega \approx 0.11$~bohr$^{-1}$ (HSE06; the project inputs set \texttt{input\_dft = 'hse'} and
use the Quantum ESPRESSO defaults, $\alpha = 0.25$ and $\omega = 0.106$~bohr$^{-1}$). Screening the exchange at long range mimics dielectric
screening in a solid and makes the method affordable with plane waves. The cost is in the exact-exchange term, which
couples every pair of occupied orbitals at all pairs of $k$ and $k+q$ points; the project therefore used a reduced $q$
grid and treated its effect as an uncertainty (\cref{sec:dft-hse}).

\paragraph{HSE06 in this project.} At the PBE geometry and with SG15 norm-conserving pseudopotentials, the bulk HSE06
gap is 2.108~eV with the $q$ grid equal to $\Gamma$ only and 41.8~meV lower with a $3\times3\times3$ $q$ grid
\fact{031}, against 0.9204~eV for NC-PBE in the same setup \fact{032}. HSE06 opens the gap by a factor of 2.3 but stays
below the experimental range. One difference from experiment is known: the calculation uses the PBE lattice, 1.87\,\%
larger than the measured one \fact{001}; how much of the remaining difference this explains was not computed. The quantity the device model needs,
the confinement shift, changes much less: the ratio $r = \Delta\Eg^{\mathrm{HSE}}/\Delta\Eg^{\mathrm{PBE}}$ of the 1~nm
slab is 1.060 ($\Gamma$-only $q$) to 1.107 (worst case with the $q$ uncertainty) \fact{030}. Why the ratio is close to
one is discussed in \cref{sec:mono-conf-hse}.

\section{Periodic solids: Bloch states and Brillouin-zone sampling}
\label{sec:mono-dft-bloch}

For a lattice-periodic potential, $v(\vr+\vR) = v(\vr)$, the Hamiltonian commutes with every lattice translation
$\hat T_{\vR}$. The translations commute with each other, so the eigenstates can be chosen as simultaneous eigenstates,
$\hat T_{\vR}\psi = c(\vR)\psi$ with $c(\vR+\vR') = c(\vR)c(\vR')$ and $|c|=1$; hence $c(\vR) = e^{i\vk\cdot\vR}$ and
\begin{equation}
\psi_{n\vk}(\vr) = e^{i\vk\cdot\vr}u_{n\vk}(\vr), \qquad u_{n\vk}(\vr+\vR) = u_{n\vk}(\vr)
\label{eq:mono-dft-bloch}
\end{equation}
(Bloch's theorem \citep{Bloch1929}). Every density or energy becomes an average over the first Brillouin zone,
$n(\vr) = \sum_n \frac{\Omega}{(2\pi)^3}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}} f_{n\vk}|\psi_{n\vk}(\vr)|^2 d\vk$, which is replaced by a
weighted sum over a finite grid. Monkhorst--Pack grids \citep{Monkhorst1976} integrate smooth periodic functions with
an error that falls exponentially with the grid density, because the Fourier components of a smooth function of $\vk$
decay exponentially. For an insulator the integrands are smooth and small grids suffice: the 40-atom primitive cell of
bixbyite (Ia$\bar3$, $a = 10.306$~\AA{} in PBE) converged its energy within 1~mRy/atom, its pressure within 1~kbar and
its gap within 10~meV on a $3\times3\times3$ grid (\cref{sec:dft-calc-conv}). For a metal the occupation jumps at the
Fermi surface, the integrand is discontinuous and the error falls only algebraically with the grid. The W-doped cells
of this project are metals in the calculation (one W donates three electrons into the conduction band), which is why
they need smearing.

\section{Occupations and smearing}
\label{sec:mono-dft-smearing}

Replacing the step occupation by a smooth function of width $\sigma$, $f_{n\vk} = \tilde f((\varepsilon_{n\vk}-\mu)/\sigma)$,
makes the integrand smooth at the price of a bias. Each choice corresponds to a generalised entropy and a free energy
$F = E - \sigma S$ that is variational, so forces are derivatives of $F$, not of $E$. Four choices matter here.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Fermi--Dirac:} $\tilde f(x) = 1/(e^x+1)$ with $\sigma = \kT$; physical at temperature $T$, but its long
tails require large widths for convergence.
\item \emph{Gaussian:} $\tilde f(x) = \tfrac12\,\mathrm{erfc}(x)$. Expanding the band energy around $\mu$ shows that
$E(\sigma) = E_0 + a\sigma^2 + O(\sigma^4)$ and $F(\sigma) = E_0 - a\sigma^2 + O(\sigma^4)$ with the same $a$, so
$E_0 \approx \tfrac12[E(\sigma)+F(\sigma)]$; occupations stay in $[0,1]$ and are monotonic.
\item \emph{Methfessel--Paxton} \citep{Methfessel1989}: Hermite-polynomial corrections remove the $\sigma^2$ term of the
energy, at the price of occupations that leave $[0,1]$.
\item \emph{Marzari--Vanderbilt (``cold'')} \citep{Marzari1999}: the broadened delta function
$\tilde\delta(x) = \pi^{-1/2}e^{-(x-a)^2}(2-\sqrt2\,x)$ with $a = 1/\sqrt2$ gives, on integration with $y = x-a$,
\begin{equation}
\tilde f(x) = \tfrac12\,\mathrm{erfc}(y) - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-y^2} .
\label{eq:mono-dft-mv}
\end{equation}
The energy error is $O(\sigma^3)$, but for $y > 1/\sqrt2$ the asymptotic form $\tfrac12\mathrm{erfc}(y)\approx
e^{-y^2}/(2\sqrt\pi\,y)$ is smaller than the second term, so $\tilde f$ is slightly \emph{negative} above the Fermi
level. MV weights are therefore numerical integration weights, not occupations of states.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Consequences for the observables of this project.} The widths used are 0.01~Ry (136~meV, MV, crystalline W
cells and the amorphous W relaxations), 0.005~Ry (68~meV, Gaussian, vacancy cells and rescued amorphous chains),
0.002~Ry (27~meV) and 0.001~Ry (13.6~meV, Gaussian reference finals), against $\kT = 25.85$~meV at 300~K. Three
consequences follow.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Any quantity that counts electrons in states within a few $\sigma$ of $\mu$ depends on $\sigma$ and on the family.
The W-5d counts of the crystalline cells (0.09--0.43~e, \fact{045}) are of this kind: they are L\"owdin projections of
MV-weighted states and are not a W charge.
\item A classification of shallow states (is a level 0.1~eV above or below $E_F$ occupied?) is meaningful only for
$\sigma$ well below the energy differences involved. This is the reason for the width audit (A15) and the 0.001~Ry
reference finals (A26) of the amorphous campaign, and for computing occupations with the final SCF's own (Gaussian)
smearing function (register section~1).
\item Geometries relaxed with different families can differ. The registered screen found that switching MV 0.01~Ry to
Gaussian 0.005~Ry at one relaxed 1.0~nm W geometry changes no force component by more than $2.4\times10^{-4}$~Ry/bohr
\claim{C034}, below the $10^{-3}$~Ry/bohr relaxation threshold, so at that geometry the family is not a geometry
uncertainty; the polish control C4 tests the same question on the endpoints (\cref{sec:mono-am-hn}).
\end{enumerate}

For the crystalline vacancy cells the smearing affects total energies, but the corrections cancel in the registered
differences: the W cells carry width corrections of 11.7--12.6~meV each, and the differences move by only 0.5 and
0.2~meV between 0.005 and 0.002~Ry \claim{C038}.
