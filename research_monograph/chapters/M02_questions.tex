\chapter{Research questions, competing hypotheses and evidence governance}
\label{ch:mono-questions}

This chapter states what the project asks, which answers compete, and the rules under which evidence for or against
them is produced and labelled. The rules are part of the results: a number in this monograph means what it means only
together with its label, the time at which its test was registered, and what was known when it was registered.

% =====================================================================================================================
\section{The central question and its parts}
\label{sec:mono-q-central}

\emph{What sets the thickness dependence of ultrathin W-doped \InO{} transistors, and can a device model with one shared
parameter set and inputs computed from first principles account for it?} The question has four parts, each with its own
evidence chain:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Electronic structure against thickness.} How do the gap, the band edges and the electron mass of an \InO{}
film change from bulk to below 1~nm, and how much of the change is in the conduction band?
(\cref{ch:dft,ch:mono-confinement-theory,ch:mono-masses})
\item \emph{The role of tungsten.} Does W give its electrons to extended host states, in the crystal and in amorphous
films, and does that change with thickness? Does it suppress oxygen vacancies? (\cref{ch:mono-w-vac,ch:mono-amorph})
\item \emph{Device electrostatics and transport.} Which mechanisms set the threshold, the swing, the on-current and the
off-state floor of each film, and which of them can a shared model reproduce?
(\cref{ch:calibration,ch:6p3,ch:mechanisms,ch:v2,ch:mono-c10})
\item \emph{Provenance.} How do the metrics derived here relate to the published ones, and what do the data permit?
(\cref{ch:mono-provenance,ch:mono-audit})
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Prerequisites.} Three questions precede every hypothesis and none is answered by the available data
(\dlog{D-11}: no new measurements): whether the project and the paper define mobility, swing, $\Cox$ and $\Vd$ the same
way (partly answered, \cref{ch:mono-provenance}); how large the device-to-device spread is (needs at least ten devices
per thickness); and how the thicknesses were measured and whether the four devices share a fabrication run and sweep
history.

\section{Evidence governance}
\label{sec:mono-q-gov}

\paragraph{Status labels.} Every scientific statement carries exactly one label: \prov{measured} (raw instrument data),
\prov{derived} (extracted from data by a written protocol), \prov{fitted} (adjusted to data), \prov{computed} (output of a
simulation with stated inputs), \prov{observed} (a pattern not yet quantified by a protocol),
\prov{literature-supported}, \prov{inferred}, \prov{hypothesis}, \prov{unresolved} or \prov{falsified}. A label is never
promoted without new evidence, and every change is recorded in the claims ledger (\file{03_CLAIMS_LEDGER.yaml}, claims
C001--C040). The monograph adds the labels \prov{assumed}, \prov{estimated}, \prov{calculated} and \prov{theory} where the
sources use them, and labels its own analyses \prov{derived}.

\paragraph{Fitting and prediction rules.} No target-device-specific post hoc fitting: global parameters may be calibrated
on a declared set, after which the model is frozen and predictions for new thicknesses or devices are registered before
the data are seen; anything tuned after a holdout is revealed is calibration. A prediction is registered with numerical
values, uncertainties and pass/fail criteria, and timestamped outside the repository before a custodian releases the
holdout. At present no holdout data exist (\dlog{D-11}), so every device-level comparison in this project is
retrospective, and the register says so.

\paragraph{The register.} The hypothesis register was frozen on 2026-10-10T18:33:13Z (\dlog{D-17}, git tag
\texttt{register-rev2-frozen-20261010T1833Z}). Every later change is an amendment logged with its time, with what had been
seen at that time, with the independent reviewer's verdict and with the user's approval; amendments that were informed by
results say so (A38, A42, A43). The register distinguishes registered predictions (scored) from forecasts (recorded, not
scored), and applies a censoring rule: a quantity below the demonstrated numerical resolution counts as consistent with an
upper bound only if that resolution is below the bound; otherwise it is unresolved, never a pass.

\paragraph{Blindness and sealing.} Outcomes of tests registered before they were known are kept out of sight until their
release conditions are met: amorphous electronic quantities until the conditional release decision \dlog{D-43} is
cleared; device-simulation stages from S2 on under a state-only reporting rule for sealed runs. Disclosed exposures are
recorded where they happened (the A36 erratum, \cref{tab:mono-corr-amorph}).

\paragraph{Roles and independence.} Claude Code executes computations, keeps the evidence and the ledger; Codex reviews
independently, first from the evidence part of a handoff and only then from its interpretation; the humans decide. The
charter states explicitly that agreement between AI systems is not evidence; experiments, registered predictions and the
judgement of human experts are. AI agents may not start expensive calculations, commit to fabrication campaigns, release
data, push to a new remote or put claims into a manuscript without explicit approval. The charter is itself proposed and
awaits adoption (\dlog{D-10}).

\paragraph{Mismatches as findings.} Decision \dlog{D-06} fixes that a mismatch between model and data is reported as a
finding, and that no window, device or floor treatment is chosen to manufacture agreement. The 13.2~nm swing mismatch is
the standing example (\claim{C010}).

\begin{keyresult}
The project asks what sets the thickness dependence of ultrathin IWO transistors and whether one shared model with
computed inputs accounts for it, through four evidence chains (electronic structure, tungsten, device physics,
provenance). Its competing hypotheses are registered with frozen criteria and labelled by status; most registered
outcomes are sealed at the evidence state of this monograph, every device comparison is retrospective, and agreement
between AI agents is explicitly not counted as evidence.
\end{keyresult}
