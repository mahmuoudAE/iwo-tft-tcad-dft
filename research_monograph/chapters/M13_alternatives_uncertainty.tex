\chapter{Alternative explanations and the uncertainty budget}
\label{ch:mono-alternatives}

A single explanation that fits the data is not evidence that it is the right one when several others fit equally well.
This chapter lists, for every observation the project relies on, the explanations that remain open, the evidence for
and against each, and the measurement or calculation that would separate them. It then collects the sources of
uncertainty in one budget. The priors quoted are the subjective first-pass priors of the research-question file
(\file{research_record/02_RESEARCH_QUESTIONS.md}); they are stated so that they can be argued with, not as evidence.

\section{The non-uniform thickness dependence}
\label{sec:mono-alt-tc}

\paragraph{Observation.} From 2.0 to 6.3~nm the threshold moves by $-0.018$~V, the peak $\muFE$ by a factor 0.9 and the
on-current by 0.79; from 6.3 to 13.2~nm by $-0.561$~V, 4.6 and 7.6; the 31.8~nm device does not switch off
(\claim{C026}, \claim{C029}; one device per film). The research-question file states this as hypothesis H1: a critical
thickness separates two regimes, and W shifts it (\claim{C027}, \prov{hypothesis}).

\section{The 6.3~nm anomaly}
\label{sec:mono-alt-6p3}

\paragraph{Observation.} Every out-of-sample threshold prediction for 6.3~nm failed, by $-0.29$, $-0.37$ and
$-0.23$~V (\claim{C014}); a monotonic confinement law implies a threshold 0.2~V below the measured one
(\cref{sec:mono-ms-explain}); the 6.3~nm film has the lowest peak mobility and a much stronger gate dependence of the
mobility than the 13.2~nm film ($\gamma = 1.13$ against 0.27; \claim{C011}, \claim{C012}).

\paragraph{Explanations.} A priori more likely: device-to-device spread, a thickness error, a different fabrication
batch, or sweep history. Less likely: real non-monotonic electrostatics (H3, prior low, \claim{C028}). The V1
hypothesis study tested six mechanisms at 6.3~nm and none explained the miss; it decomposed the miss into a rigid
0.294~V shift and a 0.16--0.19~V shape part (\cref{ch:6p3}). A thickness error would need the ``6.3~nm'' film to be
electrostatically much thinner; with $d\dEc/dt = -0.012$~eV/nm at 6.3~nm, no plausible thickness error explains 0.2~V
through confinement alone, so a thickness error would have to act through the donor or trap distribution. The
registered campaign treats the 6.3~nm residual as unexplained (H-F) and reports its shift/shape decomposition rather
than fitting it. \textbf{Decisive:} at least ten devices at 2, 4, 6.3, 9 and 13~nm from one run; H3 is falsified if
6.3~nm lies on a smooth trend within the spread.

\section{Swing, mobility and contacts}
\label{sec:mono-alt-transport}

\paragraph{Swing collapse.} The collapse of the local swing against $\Id/\Ion$ (\claim{C007}) is consistent with
surface-dominated tail states whose areal density hardly depends on thickness (H7, prior medium); it is falsified if
the collapse fails within one composition when more devices are measured.

\paragraph{Mobility.} The computed mass increase alone gives a band-mobility ratio of about 0.83 between 2.0 and 13.2~nm,
about 14\,\% of the logarithmic drop that the calibrated models need (H-D forecast; \cref{sec:mono-c10-design}); interface scattering $\propto1/t$ (H-D), percolation in disordered films
(IGZO.TOKYO branch) and contact effects are the candidates. A thickness-independent series resistance cannot create the
trend (it would need 83\,\% of $\Vd$ at 2~nm, \claim{C019}); a thickness-dependent barrier is not excluded (H5). The
definitional differences of \cref{ch:mono-provenance} must be removed before any mobility comparison with the paper.

\section{First-principles alternatives}
\label{sec:mono-alt-dft}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{W as a donor.} Computed: resonant level near $E_F$ at very high filling. Alternatives at device density: a
hybrid functional lowers the level below the host edge; a polaronic W state; compensation by W-related acceptors. Tests:
\cref{tab:mono-w-open}.
\item \emph{Vacancies.} The neutral-cell energy differences mix band filling with chemistry; charged-cell formation
energies with corrections could reverse the conclusion at device Fermi levels (\cref{sec:mono-defect-formation}).
\item \emph{Confinement.} Crystalline OH-terminated (001) slabs against amorphous films with other terminations and
interfaces; the polar 0.695~nm point; the thickness definition. H-K and H-H test part of this (sealed).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Forking paths.} Several choices were made after seeing data: the swing agreement windows (post hoc, one rule
for all films), the amendment A1 window, branch adoption in the campaign (data-selected by a rule fixed in advance),
and several register amendments labelled result-informed. The record discloses each of them, and decision D-06 forbids
choosing windows or floor treatments to manufacture agreement. A reader should nevertheless treat every agreement
between model and data in this project as calibration or as a post hoc description unless it was registered before the
outcome and passed; at the evidence state of this monograph no such test of a device-level prediction has passed.

\begin{keyresult}
For every main observation more than one explanation remains open. The non-uniform thickness dependence can be produced
by classical back-surface depletion with ordinary parameters, by a donor gradient, by structural change, by
confinement-induced donor deactivation, or by device spread, and the W-specific mechanism H-N cannot be decided by the
current campaign. The 6.3~nm anomaly is most economically explained by device spread or a fabrication difference until
device statistics exist. The off-state floor is decided by its drain-bias dependence. The largest uncertainties are not
numerical: one device per film, unknown thickness metrology, unknown stack order and the non-identifiability of the
threshold-setting quantities.
\end{keyresult}
