\chapter{Electron and hole masses and device-ready parameters}
\label{ch:mono-masses}

This chapter collects the band-structure quantities that the device model needs, as re-derived on 13--14 October 2026
from the raw outputs of every crystalline calculation, without new runs (claims \claim{C039} and \claim{C040}): the
electron masses of bulk and films on a stated fit basis, the absence of a meaningful single hole mass, the thickness
laws of the gap and band edges, and the parameters that the device decks would take at 2.0, 6.3 and 13.2~nm. Every
number is crystalline and PBE unless stated; the device films are amorphous or nanocrystalline. The theory behind the
fits is in \cref{ch:mono-confinement-theory}.

\label{sec:mono-mass-bulk}

The conduction band is a single s-like (In 5s--O 2s antibonding) band with its minimum at $\Gamma$; the valence band is
a manifold of very flat O 2p bands whose top is three-fold degenerate at $\Gamma$ (\cref{fig:mono-mass-bm1}). The
fundamental PBE gap is 0.8887~eV, indirect by 4.4~meV \fact{002}; the 0.893~eV direct gap at $\Gamma$ is not the optical
gap, because transitions from the valence-band top to the conduction band are dipole-forbidden or weak in bixbyite
\citep{Walsh2008}. The measured fundamental gap is $2.93\pm0.15$~eV \citep{King2009}; the absorption onset of melt-grown
crystals is about 2.7~eV at room temperature \citep{Irmscher2014}.

\section{Electron masses}
\label{sec:mono-mass-electrons}

\paragraph{The basis of a mass.} A conduction-band mass depends on how it is extracted. In this project three bases
occur: the band-edge mass of a 10-point Kane fit, $E(1+\alpha E) = \hbar^2k^2/2m$; a four-point parabola with free
intercept over $|k|\le0.05$~\AA$^{-1}$ (the ``deck basis'' of the device laws); and anchored parabolas over growing
energy windows. For bulk they give $0.1541\,m_0$ (Kane; isotropic within 0.2\,\%, $\alpha = 0.58$~eV$^{-1}$ with a range
0.55--0.63), $0.1588\,m_0$ (deck basis), and $0.156$ to $0.189\,m_0$ for windows of 25 to 450~meV \fact{004}
\fact{005} \fact{006}. Every mass in this monograph names its basis \corr{X6}.

\paragraph{Films.} The in-plane band-edge 2-D DOS masses are 0.443, 0.337, 0.279 and $0.203\,m_0$ at H--H thicknesses
of 0.461, 0.695, 0.930 and 1.962~nm, with in-plane non-parabolicities of 0.24, 0.31, 0.36 and 0.47~eV$^{-1}$
\claim{C039}. On the deck basis the 2~nm mass is $0.206\,m_0$; the two bases differ by $-3.0$\,\% (bulk) to $+3.4$\,\%
(0.461~nm). Checks: GPU and CPU builds agree within $0.0006\,m_0$; 15, 25 and 35~\AA{} of vacuum within $0.0002\,m_0$;
relaxation raises the 1~nm mass by 18\,\%. The law fitted to the 0.695, 0.930 and 1.962~nm points is $\Delta m =
0.1145\,t^{-1.2755}$ (band-edge basis; $t$ the H--H thickness in nm; one residual degree of freedom). As shown in
\cref{sec:mono-conf-test}, these masses follow from the computed $\dEc$ and the bulk Kane band within 2--10\,\%
\fact{027}.

\paragraph{Tungsten.} At 3.1\,\% W in bulk, the host conduction band keeps its shape and becomes 5\,\% heavier
($0.161$--$0.163\,m_0$ over directions; $\alpha$ unchanged within the direction spread) \fact{047}. In the W slabs no band
run exists; a calibrated estimate from the SCF $k$ grid gives $+6.5$\,\% (1~nm) and $+7.5$\,\% (2~nm), labelled
\prov{estimated}. A band-filling check shows what the W cells represent: with the pure-bulk Kane band, the electron
density of the cells (about $2.4$--$2.7\times10^{21}$\cmcu{} in the host band) would put $E_F$ 2.0--2.1~eV above the band
minimum, against the computed 1.71--1.76~eV; a parabolic band would put it at 4.3--4.6~eV. The Kane form removes most of
the parabolic error and still overestimates $E_F$ by 13--22\,\%, consistent with part of the donated charge sitting in
the W-5d-rich band (\cref{ch:mono-w-vac}).

\paragraph{Hybrid functional.} No HSE06 band run exists. A three-point Kane fit to the HSE06 SCF-grid energies,
calibrated on the PAW-PBE grid of the same cell, suggests a bulk band-edge mass of $0.159$--$0.193\,m_0$ and a 1~nm mass
of $0.283$--$0.293\,m_0$; the range is a model sensitivity, not an uncertainty bound, and the HSE06 confinement increment
of the mass is unresolved (\prov{estimated}, \claim{C039}).

\paragraph{Comparison with experiment.} The measured band-edge mass is $0.208\pm0.006\,m_0$ at low density
\citep[p.~225102-1]{Stokey2021} and $0.18\,m_0$ extrapolated to zero density from plasma frequencies \citep{Feneberg2016};
PBE gives $0.154\,m_0$, consistent with the general underestimate of masses by semi-local functionals. At degenerate
filling the relevant mass is the conductivity (optical) mass $m(1+2\alpha E_F)$ of the Kane band, $0.39\,m_0$ at
$10^{21}$\cmcu{} with the PBE parameters, close to the $0.4\,m_0$ reported at that density \citep{Feneberg2016}.

\section{Holes: no single band-edge mass}
\label{sec:mono-mass-holes}

In PBE the valence-band top of \InO{} is three-fold degenerate at $\Gamma$ with a camel back: the global VBM lies
4.44~meV above $\Gamma$ at 0.122~\AA$^{-1}$ along $\langle100\rangle$ (longitudinal mass $4.03\pm0.14\,m_0$), and a second
maximum lies 1.62~meV above $\Gamma$ at 0.175~\AA$^{-1}$ along $\langle110\rangle$ ($17.8\,m_0$); both are far below
$\kT$ \fact{062}. Directional curvature masses near $\Gamma$ are 3.2--3.3 ([100]), 4.3--4.6 ([110]) and 7.2--7.9~$m_0$
([111]) for band 174 and 6.9--19.8~$m_0$ for band 175, while band 176 rises along [100] and [110]. No single band-edge
hole mass is therefore meaningful \claim{C040}.

The quantity a device model can use is the valence effective density of states. The Boltzmann integral of the computed
valence density of states (saturated in the energy window; $k$-mesh convergence not established) gives
$N_V(300~\mathrm{K}) = 1.6$--$2.7\times10^{21}$\cmcu{} over four grids and two VBM references, i.e.\ a DOS-equivalent mass
$m_{h,\mathrm{DOS}} = (N_V/2.509\times10^{19})^{2/3} = 16$--$22\,m_0$ \fact{060} \fact{061}; HSE06 on the same grid gives
0.71--0.93 times the PBE value. The device decks use $N_V = 10^{19}$\cmcu{} (\prov{assumed}), which corresponds to
$0.54\,m_0$, two orders of magnitude below the computed value. Because the decks solve for electrons only, $N_V$ enters
through the intrinsic density: at 2~nm $n_i$ rises from $5.0\times10^{-10}$ to $6.7\times10^{-9}$\cmcu{}, and the
equilibrium SRH generation bound is $3.4\times10^{-3}$~cm$^{-3}$s$^{-1}$, negligible against any measured current.
Two caveats limit the use of $N_V$. Hybrid-functional work predicts that holes self-trap in \InO{} \citep{Varley2012},
so a free-hole band integral does not describe hole transport or equilibrium hole populations; and only the 0.695~nm
film gives a resolved local hole curvature (5.9~[100] and 3.3~$m_0$~[110]), on an OH-derived band of a polar film with the
next band 29~meV lower.

\Cref{tab:mono-mass-laws} gives the laws. Three points matter for their use. (i) The gap law is well determined: its
three points lie within 0.5\,\% of the law, and adding the 0.461~nm point changes the prediction at 2~nm by less than
1~meV. (ii) The conduction-band law is not: with the polar 0.695~nm point the exponent is 1.32 with a leave-one-out range
of 0.76--1.48; without it, 1.48. At 6.3~nm, the thickness of the held-out device, the options give
$\dEc = 0.050$--$0.075$~eV \fact{025}, partly outside the band 0.046--0.062~eV configured for the campaign's sensitivity
stage (\cref{ch:mono-c10}). (iii) All laws are crystalline (001) PBE with one OH termination; their extrapolation to 6.3
and 13.2~nm assumes that the power law holds beyond the computed range.

\paragraph{Absolute gaps.} PBE gaps are not used as absolute values. Three anchored estimates are available
(\prov{calculated} under the \prov{assumed} rule that the HSE/PBE ratio $r = 1.06$--$1.11$ of the 1~nm film holds at
every thickness): HSE-corrected ($\Eg^{\mathrm{HSE,bulk}} + r\Delta\Eg^{\mathrm{PBE}}$), 2.44--2.46~eV at 2~nm and 3.06~eV
at 1~nm; experiment-anchored ($2.93 + r\Delta\Eg^{\mathrm{PBE}}$), 3.28--3.30~eV at 2~nm; and the deck basis
($3.05 + \Delta\Eg$, with the 3.05~eV optical gap of a different sputtered IWO device, \citealp{Fan2021}), 3.38~eV at 2~nm.
The deck anchor is an IWO optical gap, the experimental anchor an \InO{} fundamental gap; they differ by 0.12~eV, which
is a statement about two different measured quantities, not an uncertainty of either.

\paragraph{Reproduction of the decks.} The campaign decks (C10, runs 0061, 0068 and 0069) are reproduced to at most
0.11~meV in $\Eg$ and affinity, $0.0002\,m_0$ in $m^*$ and 0.1\,\% in $\Nc$ (quasi-2-D at 2.0~nm: deck
$1.373\times10^{19}$ against $1.374\times10^{19}$\cmcu{}). The V2 deck differs by 3--5~meV because V2 used two-point laws
at the Lin-label thicknesses \corr{X12}.

\paragraph{Not in the decks.} (i) The conduction-band non-parabolicity, which raises $\Nc$ by 5.7\,\% (3-D) or 2.5\,\%
(quasi-2-D) and the conductivity mass at degenerate filling (\cref{eq:mono-conf-nc3d}). (ii) The HSE ratio $r$ in
$\Delta\Eg$ ($+6$ to $+11$\,\%). (iii) The basis difference of $m^*$ ($-3$\,\% in bulk to $+3$\,\% in the thinnest film).
(iv) The computed $N_V$ ($\approx2\times10^{21}$ against the assumed $10^{19}$\cmcu). (v) The mass for tunnelling through
the gate oxide or into the contacts, which is not computed: the slab conduction band is quantised along $z$, and the
evanescent mass in the gap needs a complex-band calculation; any use is \prov{assumed}. (vi) The W donor, which the decks
treat as fully ionised; this holds only if its resonant level stays far above $\Ec$ at device densities
(\prov{inferred}, \cref{ch:mono-w-vac}). The OH-terminated films have no state in the gap, so ideal termination adds no
interface trap; the real interfaces are not modelled by these cells.

\begin{keyresult}
PBE gives a bulk electron band-edge mass of $0.154\,m_0$ (Kane, $\alpha = 0.58$~eV$^{-1}$), 26\,\% below the measured
$0.208\,m_0$; film masses rise to $0.203\,m_0$ at 1.96~nm and $0.279\,m_0$ at 0.93~nm, a rise explained by the bulk
non-parabolicity. W at 3.1\,\% makes the host band 5\,\% heavier. There is no meaningful single hole mass: the valence-band
top is degenerate with a camel back, and the computed $N_V \approx 2\times10^{21}$\cmcu{} is about 200 times the deck
value, with negligible effect on the electron-only device simulations. The gap law is well determined
($0.815\,t^{-1.316}$~eV); the conduction-band law is not ($p = 1.32$--1.48, $\dEc(6.3~\mathrm{nm}) = 0.050$--0.075~eV). The
campaign decks reproduce the laws exactly; non-parabolicity, the HSE ratio and the computed $N_V$ are not in them.
\end{keyresult}
