Indium oxide transistors with channels a few nanometres thick are candidates for transistors built above the
interconnect of integrated circuits, where only low process temperatures are allowed. Thinning changes the material:
quantum confinement opens the gap, surfaces and disorder dominate, and dopants may behave differently. This monograph
reports a research programme on tungsten-doped indium oxide (IWO) transistors with 2.0, 6.3, 13.2 and 31.8~nm channels,
fabricated and published by a collaborating group, combining a registered extraction of their transfer curves,
drift--diffusion device simulations from per-film calibrations to a shared-parameter campaign, density-functional
calculations of crystalline \InO{} films from 0.46 to 1.96~nm, of W in crystalline cells and of oxygen vacancies, and a
registered campaign on amorphous films from 0.7 to 2.0~nm. Every statement carries a provenance label, and tests were
registered before their outcomes where possible.

The device models reproduce each film only by calibration; every out-of-sample prediction of the 6.3~nm threshold failed
($-0.23$ to $-0.37$~V), because the measured threshold stays flat between 2.0 and 6.3~nm where the computed confinement
implies a 0.2~V change. In crystalline films the PBE gap opens by 0.34~eV at 1.96~nm and 0.90~eV at 0.93~nm (HSE06/PBE
ratio 1.06--1.11 at 1~nm), mostly in the conduction band, and the film electron mass rises from $0.154$ to
$0.203$--$0.279\,m_0$; this rise follows, without fitting, from the bulk non-parabolicity at the computed subband energy,
so the device model carries one independent first-principles thickness law. In crystalline cells W is a resonant donor
whose 5d-rich level lies 1.2--1.8~eV above the host band at the computed filling; an earlier deep-donor reading was a
band-counting artefact. W raises the energy of a neutral oxygen vacancy in the degenerate crystal, but the registered
vacancy test is unresolved overall. The machine-learned amorphous structures failed their registered force check, and
the central amorphous hypothesis, a thickness-dependent loss of W donor efficacy, cannot return a crossover verdict under
its frozen rules. Its per-cell results and all later device-campaign outcomes are sealed. The decisive next steps are
measurements: the drain-bias dependence of the off-state floor, device statistics and thickness metrology.
