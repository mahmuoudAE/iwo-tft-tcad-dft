\chapter{Multiscale integration: from first-principles quantities to device parameters}
\label{ch:mono-multiscale}

The project links two levels of description: Kohn--Sham calculations of crystalline films a few unit cells thick, and
drift--diffusion simulations of transistors 20~\textmu m long. This chapter states what passes between them, with which
provenance label, which assumptions the passage introduces, and how much each assumption matters for the quantity the
device data constrain most directly, the threshold voltage. It ends with what the transfer can and cannot explain.

Three features of the chain deserve emphasis. First, only differences between films and bulk are taken from PBE; the
absolute gap, affinity and mass come from literature measurements, so the PBE gap error does not enter directly.
Second, the laws are fitted to crystalline points and evaluated at the nominal device thicknesses; the 6.3 and 13.2~nm
values are extrapolations. Third, the masses and the conduction-band shift are not independent inputs: within 2--10\,\%
the mass increase follows from $\dEc$ and the bulk non-parabolicity (\cref{sec:mono-conf-test}), so the chain carries
effectively one first-principles thickness law, $\dEc(t)$, plus the convention for $\Nc$.

\section{How much each assumption matters for the threshold}
\label{sec:mono-ms-budget}

The affinity shift moves the threshold rigidly and one-to-one in the model; the registered affinity-only runs test
exactly this (\cref{tab:mono-c10-hyp}). The other inputs act through the electron statistics, whose effect on the
threshold is amplified by the filling of the acceptor tail (a slope factor of about 3.5 at $10^{-9}$~A/\textmu m,
\amend{A13}). \Cref{tab:mono-ms-budget} lists the resulting sensitivities.

The ranking is clear: the largest first-principles-related effect on the model threshold is not a property of the
crystal at all but the statistical convention used to turn a mass into a density of states at 2~nm; next come the
thickness definition and the functional; the fit options and mass details are minor. Every one of these is small
compared with the quantities the model must explain (\cref{sec:mono-ms-explain}), but the convention effect
amounts to a third to two thirds of the 6.3~nm misses (0.23--0.37~V).

\section{What the first-principles inputs can and cannot explain}
\label{sec:mono-ms-explain}

\paragraph{Between 2.0 and 13.2~nm.} The computed conduction-band shift differs by $-0.257$~eV between 2.0 and 13.2~nm,
with a spread below 2~meV over all fit options. Transferred rigidly, it would account for 44\,\% of the measured
threshold difference of $-0.579$~V. In the full model the other inputs partly cancel it: the register's audit forecasts
a net all-inputs-off counterfactual share of about 18\,\% (\amend{A13}; a model counterfactual, not an identified
physical contribution). Either way, confinement as computed is a substantial part of, but not the whole, threshold step,
and the rest is carried by fitted charges and traps.

\paragraph{Between 2.0 and 6.3~nm.} Here the computed shift implies a threshold change of $-0.21$ to $-0.22$~V, whereas
the measured change is $-0.018$~V. A model with a monotonic confinement law therefore places the 6.3~nm threshold about
0.2~V too low, unless something else at 6.3~nm compensates. The three failed out-of-sample predictions at 6.3~nm all
missed in this direction and by a similar amount ($-0.29$, $-0.37$ and $-0.23$~V, \claim{C014}). The arithmetic does not
identify a cause; it shows that the 6.3~nm anomaly is equivalent to the 6.3~nm device behaving, electrostatically, as if
it were much thinner, or as if an additional $+0.2$~V of threshold-setting charge were present only in that device. The
candidates (device-to-device spread, a thickness error, a different fabrication batch or sweep history, a non-monotonic
property of the films) are weighed in \cref{ch:mono-alternatives}. \Cref{fig:mono-threshold-vs-dft} shows the
comparison.

\paragraph{Identifiability.} From transfer curves alone the gate work function, the bulk affinity, a uniform fixed
charge and the confinement shift act as one number per film (\claim{C018}). The model can absorb any confinement law
into $\Delta V_0$ for one film; it cannot do so with one shared value for three films, which is why the shared campaign
is the first test in which the first-principles inputs can fail. Measurements that would separate them are a flat-band
voltage against thickness from capacitance--voltage curves, the optical gap against thickness, and photoemission band
offsets (\cref{ch:mono-conclusions}).

\section{Recommendations for the transfer}
\label{sec:mono-ms-reco}

From the analysis above (monograph recommendations, not decisions):
\begin{enumerate}
\item Transfer one thickness law, $\dEc(t)$, with its fit-option band as a stated sensitivity, and derive the in-plane
mass from it through the Kane relation rather than fitting a second law.
\item State the $\Nc$ convention with every fitted charge and trap density, as the register requires, and report the
threshold sensitivity to it; at 2~nm it is the largest model-side input effect.
\item Do not transfer absolute gaps, a donor density, or hole parameters from the present calculations.
\item Treat the crystalline laws as a reference until H-K (amorphous confinement) is released; if the amorphous shift
differs by more than 0.05~eV, the registered affinity-only interventions give the mapping to the threshold.
\item Carry the thickness definition explicitly (H--H, mass-equivalent, nominal) and obtain the measurement method of the
device thicknesses before any thickness law is compared with device data.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{keyresult}
Only increments relative to bulk pass from first principles to the device model, anchored to literature bulk values,
and effectively only one of them is independent: the conduction-band shift, from which the mass increase follows. Its
difference between 2.0 and 13.2~nm is robust ($-0.257$~eV) and equals 44\,\% of the measured threshold step if
transferred rigidly. Between 2.0 and 6.3~nm the same law implies a 0.2~V threshold change that the measurement does not
show, matching the direction and size of every failed 6.3~nm prediction. The largest model-side sensitivity at 2~nm is the
density-of-states convention (142~mV), not a property of the crystal.
\end{keyresult}
