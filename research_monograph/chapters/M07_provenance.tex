\chapter{Experimental provenance and the reanalysis of the published metrics}
\label{ch:mono-provenance}

Every device-level statement of this monograph rests on four transfer curves measured by the fabrication group. This
chapter states where the curves come from and what is known and unknown about them, how they were analysed under a
registered protocol, and how the derived metrics relate to the values published by the fabrication group
\citep{Januar2026}. The relation matters because the published mobility and swing differ from those derived here by
factors of two, and because a model that is compared with one set of numbers can look right or wrong for reasons that
have nothing to do with physics.

% =====================================================================================================================
\section{The data and their custody}
\label{sec:mono-prov-data}

\paragraph{What exists.} One transfer curve per film for 2.0, 6.3, 13.2 and 31.8~nm IWO channels: $\Vg$ from $-3$ to
$+3$~V in 121 points of 50~mV, $\Vd = 0.7$~V, one forward sweep, drain current only (\claim{C001},
\prov{measured}). The curves were provided by the fabrication group (Prof.\ Min-Hung Lee's group, National Taiwan
University) as a spreadsheet; the project keeps the workbook unchanged
(\file{data/experimental_raw_preserved_IWO-TFT.xlsx}; source workbook sha256 \texttt{ce97eccd}\ldots) and a
row-preserving CSV extraction without digitisation (\file{data/experimental_clean.csv}). The workbook columns carry no
units: the current unit (A/\textmu m), $\Vd = 0.7$~V and $W/L = 290/20$~\textmu m are taken from the device schematic
that came with the data (\file{data/provenance_from_claude_package.json}).

\paragraph{What does not exist.} No reverse sweep, gate current, second drain bias, output curve, capacitance--voltage
curve, temperature series, stress series or second device per film. The thicknesses are nominal and the method by which
they were measured is not known; whether the four devices come from one fabrication run, and their sweep history, are
not known. The stack order assumed by the device model (\AlO{} against the channel) is not stated by the group; the
paper's own interface calculations use IWO/\HfO{} \citep{Januar2026}.

\paragraph{Ownership and permission.} The measured data belong to the fabrication group; the paper is open access
(CC~BY), but it makes the data available only on reasonable request. The four curves are nevertheless already in the
project's public repository. Permission for that, for further use in publications, and for processing by AI tools has
not been recorded (charter section~8; \cref{ch:mono-audit}). This monograph uses the curves as an internal working
document only.

\section{The registered extraction}
\label{sec:mono-prov-extraction}

Thresholds, swings and mobilities are extracted by one code for measured and simulated curves, under a protocol
registered before it was applied (commit 7a61687; amendment A1, an on-current-normalised swing window, commit 7ce7116;
\dlog{D-05}). The constant-current threshold $\Vthcc$ is the gate voltage at $10^{-9}$~A/\textmu m (log-linear
interpolation); the off-state floor is the median current between $-2$ and $-0.5$~V; the field-effect mobility is
$\muFE = L\gm/(W\Cox\Vd)$ with the geometric $\Cox = 8.955\times10^{-7}$~F/cm$^2$ of 15~nm \HfO{} ($\varepsilon = 19.57$)
in series with 2~nm \AlO{} ($\varepsilon = 9.0$, assumed). An independent re-implementation for this monograph, written
from the protocol text and not importing the project code, reproduces every core number within rounding
(\cref{tab:mono-prov-metrics}).

\paragraph{Observations.} Three observations follow from the curves alone (\prov{observed}, one device per film). The
thickness dependence is not uniform: from 2.0 to 6.3~nm the threshold moves by $-0.018$~V and the on-current changes
by a factor 0.79, from 6.3 to 13.2~nm by $-0.561$~V and 7.6 (\claim{C026}). The 31.8~nm device never switches off
($\Id(-3~\mathrm{V}) = 3.0\times10^{-7}$~A/\textmu m; on/off ratio about 12 over the sweep, \claim{C029}). And the local
swing of the three thinner films collapses onto one curve when plotted against $\Id/\Ion$, in measurement and in
simulation (\cref{fig:mono-prov-ss}, \claim{C007}). The off-state floors rise with thickness (local exponents 3.1 and
5.1; one power law through three points gives $t^{3.85}$, \claim{C004}) and are flat in $\Vg$ within 3\,\% at 13.2~nm,
which makes gate leakage unlikely (\claim{C005}, \prov{inferred}; no gate current was recorded).

\section{The published mobility and the saturation formula}
\label{sec:mono-prov-mobility}

The paper reports maximum mobilities falling from about 27.4 to 5.1~\cmVs{} between 13.2 and 2.0~nm
\citep[pp.~3, 7]{Januar2026} (about 3.7--4~\cmVs{} at 6.3~nm, read from its figures), falling with gate voltage. The transconductance mobility of the same curves peaks at 50.2, 10.9 and 12.1~\cmVs{} and rises up to about
3~V. The difference is a factor of 1.8 to 2.9 (\claim{C024}, \prov{unresolved}).

\paragraph{Numerical reconciliation.} The peak of the saturation-formula mobility,
$\musat = (2L/W\Cox)(d\sqrt{\Id}/d\Vg)^2$, applied to the $\Vd = 0.7$~V curves with the protocol $\Cox$ and no free
factor, gives 5.09, 3.86 and 27.40~\cmVs{}, matching the paper's 5.1, 3.73 (digitised from its Fig.~1b) and 27.4
(\claim{C030}, \prov{derived}; reproduced independently by the reviewer and again here: 5.087 at 1.75~V, 3.858 at
2.55~V, 27.403 at 0.95~V, \fact{111}--\fact{113}). This makes the saturation-formula peak the leading candidate for the
paper's definition (\claim{C031}, \prov{inferred}); it is not an identification, because the three peaks constrain only
the product $\Cox\cdot W/L\cdot$(current normalisation), and two other definitions also fit with one common factor
(an effective mobility with a power-law threshold, factor 0.68; the Y-function, factor 0.66).

\paragraph{Why the formula gives lower and falling values.} In the linear regime, $\Id = \mu\Cox(W/L)(V_{ov} -
\Vd/2)\Vd$ with $V_{ov} = \Vg - \Vth$. Inserting it into the saturation formula with a constant $\mu$ gives
\begin{equation}
\musat = \frac{2L}{W\Cox}\Big(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\Id}}\frac{d\Id}{d\Vg}\Big)^2 = \mu\,\frac{\Vd}{2V_{ov}-\Vd} ,
\label{eq:mono-prov-musat}
\end{equation}
which is below $\mu$ whenever $V_{ov} > \Vd$ and falls as $1/V_{ov}$. A saturation formula applied to linear-regime
curves can therefore produce a low mobility that falls with gate voltage even when the true mobility is constant. The
formula is valid only for $V_{ov} \le \Vd$; at the peaks the overdrive is 1.09, 1.91 and 0.87~V with $\Vthcc$ as the
threshold, so all three peaks lie outside that range, and 6.3~nm lies outside it under all three threshold definitions
tested (\claim{C031}).

\paragraph{The identity does not hold quantitatively.} \Cref{eq:mono-prov-musat} predicts the ratio $\musat/\muFE$ at
the peak gate voltages: 0.47, 0.23 and 0.68 for constant mobility and $\Vthcc$. The curves give 0.72, 0.41 and 1.02
(\file{evidence/derived/musat_ratio.json}). The observed ratios are larger by a factor of 1.5 to 1.8, as expected if the
mobility rises with gate voltage, which the registered power-law fits find (exponents $\gamma = 0.27\pm0.08$ at 13.2~nm
and 1.13 at 6.3~nm, \claim{C011}, \claim{C012}). The saturation-formula peaks are therefore numerical correspondences
with the paper's numbers, not mobilities of the channels; neither they nor $\muFE$ is an intrinsic mobility.

\section{The published swing}
\label{sec:mono-prov-ss}

The paper describes the swing of the thin IWO devices as near-thermal, 60--70~mV/dec \citep[p.~3]{Januar2026}. Its own Fig.~1b gives 61.8, 87.4 and 119.3~mV/dec for
2.0, 6.3 and 13.2~nm (digitised), so 60--70~mV/dec holds at 2~nm only (\claim{C025}). The registered windows give
123.6, 122.7 and 115.3~mV/dec, up to 168\,\% higher. Of 31 scored estimator variants, 14 reproduce the paper's values
within 15\,\% when they start within 1--12 times the off-state floor (\claim{C032}, \prov{derived}); the best match is a
selection on the outcome and is not adopted. Near the floor, an additive floor current $F$ makes the measured swing
flatter, $\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{raw}} = \mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{ch}}(1+F/I_{\mathrm{ch}})$, and subtracting the floor steepens
it by construction; some near-floor estimators fall below the thermionic limit $\kT\ln10 = 59.5$~mV/dec at 300~K, which
no channel current can do. At 2~nm the steep section starts at about $1.9\times10^{-14}$~A/\textmu m (5.6~pA in total)
and spans 0.10--0.15~V; whether it is channel behaviour or instrumental is unresolved, and neither near-thermal
switching over a useful range nor a low trap density is established.

\paragraph{Off-current markers.} The paper's Fig.~1b off-current markers equal the floors derived here within factors
of 0.71, 0.93 and 1.02 (\claim{C033}); the 2~nm value differs by about two reading uncertainties, and the paper's axis
is labelled A while the workbook is read as A/\textmu m.

\begin{keyresult}
The analysis rests on four single-device curves owned by the fabrication group, already public but without recorded
permission. Under a registered protocol, independently re-implemented here, their thresholds, floors and currents are
reproduced within rounding. The published mobilities are reproduced numerically, with no free factor, by the peak of the
saturation formula, which is the leading candidate for the paper's definition but is applied outside its validity
range and does not describe these curves as a mobility. The published ``near-thermal'' swing holds at 2~nm only and is
reproduced by several near-floor estimators, none adopted. The open provenance questions can only be answered by the
fabrication group.
\end{keyresult}
