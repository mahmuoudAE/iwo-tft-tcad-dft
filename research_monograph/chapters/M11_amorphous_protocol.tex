\chapter{Amorphous films: structure generation, numerical qualification and the registered tests}
\label{ch:mono-amorph}

The device channels are sputtered, largely amorphous or nanocrystalline films \citep[p.~3]{Januar2026}, while every
first-principles number in the preceding chapters belongs to a crystal. This chapter describes the calculations
designed to close that gap: machine-learned melt--quench structures of amorphous \InO{} (\aIO) and amorphous IWO
(\aIWO) films from 0.7 to 2.0~nm and in bulk, relaxed by density-functional theory, and a set of hypotheses about them
that were registered before any electronic quantity was read. The central one, H-N, asks whether the ability of a W
atom to place its electron in an extended host state depends on film thickness.

The chapter reports the method, the numerical qualification of the calculations (all of it computed and logged) and
the frozen rules by which the electronic results will be read. It reports no electronic result of any amorphous cell:
their release is blocked by a logged human decision (\dlog{D-43}), and the monograph respects that block. One
consequence of the frozen rules can, however, be stated now, because it does not depend on any sealed value: the
registered H-N outcome will be \prov{UNRESOLVED} whatever the calculations show (\cref{sec:mono-am-a37}).

\section{Why thickness could change what a W atom does}
\label{sec:mono-am-why}

Tungsten is used in \InO{} because it donates electrons while scattering them weakly: in crystalline films W-doped
\InO{} reaches high Hall mobilities \citep{Newhouse2005}, and in amorphous In--W--O transistors a few per cent of W
suppresses excess carriers and improves stability \citep{Kizu2014}. A resonant donor whose d level lies inside the
host conduction band, as computed for W in crystalline \InO{} (\cref{ch:mono-w-vac}) and argued for Mo
\citep{Swallow2020}, gives its electron to the extended host band and leaves an ionised impurity behind. Whether a
W atom behaves in this way in a sub-nanometre amorphous film is not obvious, for at least four reasons, each of which
can be stated before any calculation (\prov{theory}; the quantitative versions are in \cref{ch:mono-defect-theory}).

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Confinement moves the host edge more than a localised level.} The host conduction-band edge of a thin film
rises roughly as a power of $1/t$ (\cref{ch:mono-confinement-theory}); a d level whose energy is fixed mainly by the
local W--O bonding rises less. If the two cross, the electron is no longer released. In the crystalline cells the
computed W-level separation from the host band bottom falls from 1.76--1.80~eV (bulk) to 1.34~eV (2~nm) and 1.22~eV
(1~nm) \fact{042}; the three cells differ in donated density ($2.7$, $1.4$ and $3.0\times10^{21}$\cmcu{}), so this
trend cannot be extrapolated, but its direction is the one this mechanism expects.
\item \emph{Weaker dielectric screening.} A film thinner than the extent of a donor's bound state, bounded by vacuum or
a low-permittivity oxide, screens the donor's Coulomb potential less than bulk does; donor binding energies then rise,
as observed for donor deactivation in silicon nanowires \citep{Diarra2007,Bjork2009}.
\item \emph{Surfaces everywhere.} In a 0.7~nm film every atom is within about 0.35~nm of a surface. Under-coordinated
cations, surface OH and H, and surface dipoles can supply states that capture the donated electron.
\item \emph{Disorder and dimensionality.} Amorphous oxides have band tails of localised states below a mobility edge
\citep{Mott1969,Medvedeva2017}. In the scaling theory of localisation, disorder localises states more readily in two
dimensions than in three \citep{Abrahams1979,Anderson1958}. A thinner film is ``more two-dimensional'', so the share of
localised states near the host edge may grow.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Why this matters for the devices.} The calibrated device models need an effective donor density that grows
steeply with thickness (V1: $N_{\mathrm{d,eff}} = 2.5\times10^{17}[1+(t/20)^4]$\cmcu{}, fitted and not identifiable,
\claim{C016}). A loss of W donor efficiency in the thinnest films would be one microscopic candidate for such a law.
It is not the only one: back-surface depletion, a growth gradient of oxygen vacancies, and structural change with
thickness predict the same sign (\cref{ch:mono-questions,ch:mono-alternatives}). Moreover, at about 2\,\% W, full
activation would give of the order of $6\times10^{20}$\cmcu{}, three orders of magnitude above the fitted effective
densities, so compensation must dominate in the devices whatever H-N shows. H-N can therefore at most identify a
mechanism that acts in the right direction; it cannot by itself explain the device data.

\section{What neutral PBE cells can and cannot show}
\label{sec:mono-am-scope}

All amorphous cells are neutral, spin-unpolarised PBE calculations without a Hubbard correction; hybrid-functional and
spin-polarised calculations enter only as pre-declared checks \amend{A23} \amend{A27}. Such cells can show that the
electron added by W ends up in extended or in localised states at the geometry and filling of the cell. They cannot
establish full ionisation in the device, a thermodynamic charge-transition level, or equilibrium self-trapping: those
need charged cells with finite-size corrections \citep{Freysoldt2014,Lany2008}, a hybrid functional for the
localisation, and structural relaxation in each charge state (\cref{ch:mono-defect-theory}). PBE also tends to
delocalise electrons through its self-interaction error, so a PBE ``extended'' result is weaker evidence than a PBE
``localised'' one. The register states these limits in the scope of H-G and H-N; the monograph keeps them.

\section{Structure generation: machine-learned melt--quench}
\label{sec:mono-am-mace}

Amorphous structures were generated with the MACE-MPA-0 ``medium'' foundation potential \citep{Batatia2022,Batatia2023}
using Langevin dynamics with a 1~fs time step and a friction of 0.01~fs$^{-1}$, single precision for the dynamics and
double precision for relaxations. For a film, a periodic box of height $t$ at the target density is pre-relaxed, held at
3000~K for 6~ps, quenched linearly to 300~K over 9~ps and held at 300~K for 2~ps; a vacuum gap is then opened along
$z$, the free slab is annealed at 800~K for 3~ps and cooled to 300~K over 3~ps, relaxed to 0.05~eV/\AA, passivated and
relaxed again. Bulk cells skip the cleave and the passivation. For an interface cell the oxide is melt--quenched first
(3000~K for 4~ps, quench over 6~ps) and then held fixed while the film is melted and quenched on it. The seed fixes
the random packing and initial velocities, but the Langevin noise comes from an unseeded generator, so a re-run of the
same specification gives a different structure. A structure enters DFT only after a quality-control gate on density,
bond lengths and coordination (\file{research_record/compute/amorph/mace_amorph.py}, \file{SLAB_PROTOCOL.md}).

\begin{caveat}
A 23~ps quench corresponds to a cooling rate of about $10^{14}$~K/s, many orders of magnitude faster than sputter
deposition. Melt--quench structures are therefore models of a disordered network of the right composition and density,
not reconstructions of the deposited films; one sample per name is computed (two at 2.0~nm), so sample-to-sample
spread is known only where two seeds exist.
\end{caveat}

\section{Slab construction (protocol revision 2)}
\label{sec:mono-am-slabs}

The slab rules were registered before any machine-learned or DFT result (\dlog{D-14}) and are applied in code.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Doping.} W/(In+W) is exactly 2.0\,\% in every IWO cell. Each W carries one extra O, which leaves one
excess electron per W by formal counting (W$^{6+}$ replacing In$^{3+}$ gives three, the extra O$^{2-}$ takes two):
In$_{49n}$W$_n$O$_{76n}$ against the undoped twin In$_{50n}$O$_{75n}$. W atoms start at least 6~\AA{} apart. One
electron per W corresponds to about $6\times10^{20}$\cmcu{}.
\item \emph{Lateral size.} The number of W per cell is the smallest that gives an in-plane edge of at least 12.5~\AA{}
at 7.0~g/cm$^3$, so the W volume density is the same at every thickness; all cells use a $2\times2\times1$ $k$ grid.
\item \emph{Thickness.} The label is the mass-equivalent thickness $t_{\mathrm{eq}}$ (film mass divided by
7.0~g/cm$^3$ and by the area); the outer-atom span, the cation span and an electronic thickness are also reported. This
differs from the H--H thickness of the crystalline slabs, so thicknesses of the two series are not interchangeable.
\item \emph{Termination.} Dissociated water (OH on an under-coordinated cation, H on an under-coordinated O, in pairs,
so the electron count is unchanged), with bare-surface controls at 2.0 and 0.7~nm.
\item \emph{Vacuum.} 18~\AA{} and a dipole correction along $z$ \citep{Bengtsson1999} for every slab.
\item \emph{Rejection.} A structure that fails quality control is replaced by the same recipe with the next seed;
thresholds are never changed after a result. Three structures were replaced.
\item \emph{Doping-regime control.} A fully compensated 2.0~nm cell with zero excess electrons (\dlog{D-15}).
\item \emph{Interfaces.} About 1~nm of amorphous \AlO{} (3.0~g/cm$^3$) melt--quenched first; an \HfO{} control at
9.6~g/cm$^3$ (\prov{assumed}) as an alternative-interface control only (\dlog{D-20}); atoms within 3~\AA{} of the lowest
substrate cation excluded from every interface weight (\amend{A24}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Validation of the machine-learned structures}
\label{sec:mono-am-mlval}

The criteria were fixed before any result (\file{research_record/claude/plans/ML_VALIDATION.md}): MACE forces on
selected snapshots are compared with DFT single-point forces (PBE, 60/480~Ry, Marzari--Vanderbilt 0.01~Ry, dipole
correction for slabs) on identical geometries, with limits of 0.15~eV/\AA{} for the force RMSE, 0.10~eV/\AA{} for the
MAE and 0.25~eV/\AA{} for the RMSE on W and its first-shell O; melt frames are reported, not scored. The comparator was
frozen in a commit before any output was read.

The check is \textbf{failed overall}: the undoped bulk passes; the W-doped bulk fails on the W first shell only; the
0.7~nm W slab fails all three limits (\cref{tab:mono-am-mace}). Between 71 and 75\,\% of the squared error sits in the
W first shell, which contains one W environment (7 atoms, 21 components) per frame; outside it the RMSE is
0.063--0.066 (bulk) and 0.107~eV/\AA{} (0.7~nm). The final frames are MACE-relaxed (MACE force RMS
0.005--0.013~eV/\AA), so their RMSE essentially measures the DFT residual force at the MACE minimum and tests how well
MACE equilibrium geometries transfer to DFT; the quenched and melt frames test force agreement over a wide range
($r = 0.98$--0.99). The two 2.0~nm frames were pending at the time of logging and cannot change the overall verdict.

Under the pre-registered consequence, the machine-learned structures are DFT-relaxed starting points, not validated
amorphous models, and no structural statistic from the machine-learning stage alone is reported \corr{X8}. A possible
contributor is that the potential's training data treat W oxides with PBE+U while the reference here is plain PBE
(\prov{assumed}, not tested). The practical consequence is that every structural statement about the W environment
must come from the DFT-relaxed geometry, and the W first shell is exactly where the starting geometry is least
trustworthy.

\section{DFT relaxation and continuation rules}
\label{sec:mono-am-relax}

Relaxations use 60/480~Ry, BFGS with $10^{-3}$~Ry/bohr and $10^{-4}$~Ry, at most 400 ionic steps, a
$2\times2\times1$ (slabs) or $2\times2\times2$ (bulk) grid, a dipole correction for slabs, \texttt{conv\_thr}
$10^{-7}$, $\beta = 0.2$, local-TF mixing and at most 200 SCF iterations, on GPUs with the 7.3.1 build. Each relaxation
runs as a chain of segments (an HTCondor DAG) of at most 79\,200~s (165\,600~s for one cell, \dlog{D-41}). The
continuation rules (\dlog{D-24} and amendments) are:
\begin{itemize}
\item a segment restarts only from a \emph{validated} geometry (converged SCF, accepted ionic step), the lowest-energy
one, with only the atomic positions replaced in the input;
\item a relaxation counts as converged only if Quantum ESPRESSO prints ``bfgs converged'', because it also prints final
coordinates after a failed BFGS run (\amend{A19}, \dlog{D-22});
\item every segment ends in one of the classes converged, continue, or failed with a category; a hard failure is not
replayed, and a stopped segment is held for review (\dlog{D-27});
\item a fatal GPU error after a new validated geometry is an infrastructure failure with the geometry archived.
\end{itemize}
To fit 250--300-atom cells on one 96~GB GPU, the In and O species labels are split into two labels each with identical
data sets (\amend{A25}, \amend{A30}, \amend{A34}). At a fixed geometry the split changed the energy by
$10^{-8}$~Ry and no force component by more than $3\times10^{-8}$~Ry/bohr, against tolerances of $10^{-5}$
(\claim{C035}; check-only \texttt{conv\_thr} $10^{-14}$). Every final SCF restores the original labels.

\section{Smearing families}
\label{sec:mono-am-smearing}

The W-doped slabs were registered to relax with Marzari--Vanderbilt (MV) smearing of 0.01~Ry \citep{Marzari1999};
the undoped slabs and bulk references relax with Gaussian smearing of 0.005~Ry after SCF failures of undoped slabs.
Smearing is a numerical device to integrate the partly filled bands of a metal-like cell; its theory, and why the
family and width can matter for a classification of shallow states, is given in \cref{sec:mono-dft-smearing}. The
amendments, each logged before the result it governs, are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \amend{A15} (rescue and audit): Gaussian 0.005~Ry with $\beta = 0.1$, 12 mixing vectors and at most 300
iterations; the final smearing is selected by an audit at fixed geometry (0.005, 0.002, 0.001~Ry on the 2.0~nm cells:
edges within 0.05~eV, trap classes unchanged, donated charge within five percentage points).
\item Screen at 1.0~nm: on the relaxed \file{aIWO_1p0_s1}, switching the family changed no force component by more than
$2.4\times10^{-4}$~Ry/bohr (RMS $6.7\times10^{-5}$), below the $10^{-3}$ threshold (\claim{C034}); this screen does not
replace the required Gaussian polish.
\item \amend{A26}: Gaussian 0.001~Ry reference final SCFs before the audit; self-comparison is not an audit pass.
\item \amend{A31}: the audit runs as CPU finals; because the frozen reader does not compute the electron affinity, the
audit is partial by construction and every dependent result carries ``A15 width provisional (EA not evaluated)''.
\item \amend{A35}, \amend{A36}: one-attempt Gaussian rescues of the 0.8~nm and two 2.0~nm W chains after SCF
failures.
\end{itemize}
The W series therefore uses mixed relaxation smearing, and every result table gives each cell's family and lineage.

\section{Restart reproducibility and qualification}
\label{sec:mono-am-restart}

For \file{aIWO_0p8_s2} a fresh SCF at a fixed geometry with identical settings did not reproduce the continued SCF at
production tolerance: the energy differed by $1.18\times10^{-5}$~Ry and a force component by $4.99\times10^{-3}$~Ry/bohr
(\claim{C036}). Whether this is an incomplete-SCF force error or two converged electronic branches is open.
Numerical-path invariance is therefore not assumed. For every cell on a rescue route two gates follow: a restart gate
(the first fresh SCF of a new segment must reproduce the previous value within $10^{-5}$~Ry and $10^{-3}$~Ry/bohr) and
a qualification SCF at the final geometry with the same tolerances. A failed or missing qualification makes the
relaxation \emph{exploratory}: the frozen reader excludes it from primary scoring, and the flag is inherited by every
geometry derived from it. Any relaxation not converged at the cutoff is exploratory, and a primary test without a
converged geometry is unresolved.

\section{Final SCF, export and certification}
\label{sec:mono-am-final}

Final SCFs run on CPUs (QE 7.5) at 71/568~Ry, the cutoff of the crystalline study, with $1.3\times$ the occupied band
count, followed by the planar-averaged potential and \texttt{projwfc.x} projections. Exported before scratch space is
lost: per-state projections with atom and orbital maps and completeness values, projected densities of states,
occupations and $k$ weights, planar averages, final geometries with lineage, eigenvalues, every segment's inputs and
logs. The read-out is a frozen Python package (version 1.0.0-rc7; code sha256 \texttt{fff642a1}\ldots\texttt{e4f01158};
221 tests), frozen before any slab read-out existed (\dlog{D-32}). Before use on amorphous cells it must reproduce two
crystalline numbers, a 3\,\% W-5d share and $\dEc = 0.281$~eV at 2~nm. For each final a certificate binds by sha256
every file the read-out consumes, the geometry and pseudopotential hashes, and one block per qualification; an
automated checker (F9) recomputes every hash. Without a valid certificate every primary score is unresolved.

\section{Read-out definitions}
\label{sec:mono-am-defs}

The definitions were frozen with the register (section 1, amended by A16 and A24 before any electronic result). Their
theoretical basis is in \cref{sec:mono-defect-pr,sec:mono-defect-lowdin}. For a state $(n,\vk)$ with L\"owdin
projections $\tilde p_{i}$ summed over the orbitals of atom $i$, the atom weights are $p_i = \tilde p_i/\sum_j\tilde
p_j$, and the completeness $\sum_j \tilde p_j = 1 - s_{n\vk}$ (with $s$ the spilling) is reported; a state with
completeness below 0.90 is flagged, and a test resting on flagged states is unresolved. The normalised participation
ratio over all $N$ atoms of the cell is
\begin{equation}
\PRn = \frac{1}{N\sum_{i=1}^{N} p_i^2}, \qquad \frac{1}{N} \le \PRn \le 1 ,
\label{eq:mono-am-pr}
\end{equation}
with extended states $\PRn \ge 0.20$ and localised states $\PRn \le 0.05$ (absolute thresholds; the relative rule of
revision 1 could call a fully localised spectrum extended). $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = N\,\PRn$ is reported alongside, since
$\PRn$ scales as $1/N$ for an unchanged localised support. Occupations use the final SCF's own smearing function and
normalised $k$ weights including the spin factor.

\paragraph{Host edge.} The lowest eigenvalue over all $k$ of any state above band $N_v$ with $\PRn\ge0.20$ and an
interior weight of at least 0.8 times the interior atom fraction; unresolved if no state qualifies within 1.0~eV of the
lowest state above $N_v$ or if the interior is empty. A host valence edge is defined in the same way at or below
$N_v$. The rule does not use the W-5d weight, so the W tests are not circular.

\paragraph{Electron accounting.} $N_v = (N_{\mathrm{el}} - N_{\mathrm{ex}})/2$ with $N_{\mathrm{ex}} = n_W$ in W-doped
cells and 0 in undoped and compensated cells. The occupation of all bands above $N_v$ must equal $N_{\mathrm{ex}}$ within
0.02~e (otherwise unresolved), and every fraction uses the nominal denominator $N_{\mathrm{ex}}$.

\paragraph{Alignment.} Valid only with an interior plateau of the planar-averaged potential with a standard deviation
of at most 0.05~eV; without it no edge shift is claimed.

\section{H-N: extended-carrier efficacy of W against thickness}
\label{sec:mono-am-hn}

H-N was added after the register freeze by \amend{A27} (2026-10-11T09:09Z), before any slab electronic result was read,
after an independent review, and approved by the user (\dlog{D-28}). It is exploratory: its forecasts are not scored,
but its read-out rules are frozen.

\paragraph{Cells.} Bulk (\file{aIWO_bulk_s1} / \file{aIO_bulk_s1}); 2.0~nm (two W seeds and two undoped partners);
1.5~nm (optional; cut); 1.0~nm; 0.8~nm; 0.7~nm (one W cell and one undoped partner each).

\paragraph{Observables} (W-doped cells, final SCF, bands $n>N_v$, nominal denominator $N_{\mathrm{ex}} = n_W$):
\begin{align}
f_{\mathrm{ext}} &= \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{ex}}}\sum_{n>N_v,\vk}\, f_{n\vk}w_{\vk}\;
  \mathbf{1}\!\left[\PRn \ge 0.20,\; \varepsilon_{n\vk} \ge E_{\mathrm{host}}\right], \label{eq:mono-am-fext}\\
f_{\mathrm{locW}} &= \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{ex}}}\sum_{n>N_v,\vk}\, f_{n\vk}w_{\vk}\;
  \mathbf{1}\!\left[\PRn \le 0.05,\; p_{\mathrm{W5d}} \ge 0.30\right], \label{eq:mono-am-flocw}\\
\Delta_W &= \min\{\varepsilon_{n\vk} : p_{\mathrm{W5d}} \ge 0.30,\ \varepsilon_{n\vk} > E_{\mathrm{host,v}}\} -
E_{\mathrm{host}}, \label{eq:mono-am-dw}
\end{align}
and $f_{\mathrm{W5d}}$, the W-5d share of the excess electrons. The final band count must reach at least 1.0~eV above
the host edge at every $k$; if no qualifying W state lies within the computed bands, $\Delta_W$ is a lower bound, and it
counts as $>0.3$~eV only if that bound exceeds 0.3~eV.

\paragraph{Per-cell classes} (operational screens, not universal donor criteria):
DONOR if $f_{\mathrm{ext}}\ge0.80$, $\Delta_W>0.3$~eV and $f_{\mathrm{locW}}<0.10$; ECB (extended-carrier efficacy
breakdown) if $f_{\mathrm{ext}}<0.50$ or $f_{\mathrm{locW}}\ge0.50$; PARTIAL otherwise; UNRESOLVED on any unresolved
section-1 condition, a failed band-coverage rule or a pending smearing qualification. A low $f_{\mathrm{ext}}$ can also
mean that an ionised donor's electron sits in a host or surface trap; ECB therefore does not by itself mean that W
stopped donating. A W-centred attribution of an ECB needs the trapping states to carry $\ge30$\,\% of their weight on W
and its first-shell O, and the paired undoped parent to have no localised state within $\pm0.10$~eV on the same non-W
atoms; otherwise the ECB is host-trap-mediated or unattributed.

\section{What H-N can conclude: the consequence of A37}
\label{sec:mono-am-a37}

\amend{A37} (2026-10-13T00:10Z, before any H-N class of any cell had been computed; \dlog{D-42}) records the following
chain. The 2.0~nm seed \file{aIWO_2p0_s2} never converged in its only Marzari--Vanderbilt segment and continues on the
one-attempt Gaussian route of \amend{A36}. It therefore never has an MV-converged parent, and control C4 (``polished by a
Gaussian 0.005~Ry relax, starting from its MV geometry'') cannot be satisfied for it. No other 2.0~nm W seed exists. A
native-Gaussian eligibility amendment was declined because the relaxation-SCF Fermi energies of two 2.0~nm W cells had
been parsed before any rule allowed it (the A36 erratum, \cref{tab:mono-corr-amorph}), so restoring a scorable endpoint
would be result-exposed.

\begin{proposition}[H-N cannot return a crossover verdict]
\label{prop:mono-hn}
Under the frozen rules and \amend{A37}, the registered H-N outcome is \textsc{unresolved} in every branch.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
CROSSOVER requires both 2.0~nm seeds DONOR with C4 at 2.0~nm; NO CROSSOVER requires DONOR at 2.0~nm for both seeds with
C4. C4 is unsatisfiable for one of the two registered seeds in every branch (whatever happens to the other seed, to
the audit, or to an extension of computer access), so neither outcome is reachable. The remaining outcomes (PARTIAL,
sample-dependent, UNRESOLVED) carry no crossover claim, and A37 replaces any reader output by UNRESOLVED with the reason
``2.0~nm thick endpoint UNRESOLVED (A36 C4 point; A37 override)''. The override acts on the outcome string only, never
upgrades, and leaves per-cell classes, controls and notes intact. \qedhere
\end{proof}

The proposition follows from the rules alone and uses no sealed value. Its consequences for the research programme are
direct:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The central original question of the amorphous campaign, as registered, cannot be answered by this campaign.
Neither ``W stops donating below a critical thickness'' nor ``W keeps donating down to 0.7~nm'' will be a registered
result.
\item What can be reported after release are per-cell exploratory descriptions: the class of each resolved cell with
its controls (C1 and C6 at 0.7~nm are kept, because they qualify the 0.7~nm class and the W-centred attribution), its
smearing family, lineage and certificate.
\item A future test of H-N needs at least two independently quenched 2.0~nm W seeds that relax in one smearing family,
a registered endpoint rule that does not depend on a polish route, and the C2/C5 controls restored. The present
campaign supplies the protocol, the reader and the qualification machinery for it.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Reading guide for the exploratory per-cell results (monograph, not a registered rule).} Written on
2026-10-14 without sight of any amorphous electronic quantity, to fix in advance how the monograph will describe the
released per-cell results:
\begin{itemize}
\item DONOR at 0.7~nm with C3, C4 and C6 passing: consistent with W placing its electron in extended states of the
computed 0.7~nm network; it does not establish that W remains a donor in the devices (neutral PBE, one network).
\item ECB at 0.7~nm with a W-centred attribution confirmed by C1: a candidate for a W-specific loss of
extended-carrier efficacy at 0.7~nm in the computed networks; without C2 and C5 it can be a dilution or a functional
artefact, and without independent quenches it is restricted to the parent networks.
\item ECB, host-trap-mediated or unattributed: points to host or surface trapping, not to W chemistry.
\item PARTIAL or UNRESOLVED: no statement about W.
\end{itemize}
Whatever the classes, the comparison across thicknesses is a description of single networks with different
terminations and densities, not a trend; a sequence of classes is not a dependence on thickness without device-to-network
statistics.

\section{Gap anchoring and hybrid checks}
