\chapter{Tungsten donor states and oxygen vacancies in crystalline \texorpdfstring{\InO}{In2O3}}
\label{ch:mono-w-vac}

The published paper attributes the behaviour of the IWO films partly to tungsten: W doping ``passivates oxygen vacancies'', reducing deep traps, and ``promotes smoother charge
delocalization'', lowering the tail-state density \citep[p.~4]{Januar2026} (\claim{C023}, \prov{literature-supported}, not
tested by the paper's own crystalline DFT in this respect). This chapter reports what the project's crystalline
calculations say about both statements: where the W level lies relative to the host band and what happens to the
donated electrons (\claim{C022}), and whether W raises the energy of a neutral oxygen vacancy (\claim{C038}, register
H-L). Both are crystalline, PBE, degenerately doped references; neither tests the amorphous films, which are the
subject of \cref{ch:mono-amorph}.

% =====================================================================================================================
\section{Cells and settings}
\label{sec:mono-w-cells}

W was placed in the 80-atom conventional bixbyite cell on either cation site (In$_{31}$WO$_{48}$, 3.1\,\% W of the
cations, fixed PBE lattice $a = 10.3061$~\AA) and on a central 24d site of the relaxed 1 and 2~nm films
(In$_{23}$WO$_{48}$H$_{24}$, 4.2\,\%; In$_{55}$WO$_{96}$H$_{24}$, 1.8\,\%), with PAW-PBE at 71/568~Ry and
Marzari--Vanderbilt smearing of 0.01~Ry; bulk relaxations on $3\times3\times3$ and single points on $4\times4\times4$
grids. The donated densities, three electrons per W by formal counting, are $2.7\times10^{21}$ (bulk),
$3.0\times10^{21}$ (1~nm) and $1.4\times10^{21}$\cmcu{} (2~nm), about three orders of magnitude (1500--3300 times) above the
Mott density
(\cref{sec:mono-defect-hydrogenic}). The valence-band top of every W cell is band $n_{\mathrm{pure}}-1$
(\cref{eq:mono-dft-bands}).

\section{Site, bonding and valence}
\label{sec:mono-w-site}

W prefers the 24d site: $E(8\mathrm{b}) - E(24\mathrm{d}) = 0.2579$~eV on the $3\times3\times3$ relaxation grid and
0.2577~eV on the $4\times4\times4$ single point \fact{040}. With three times as many 24d as 8b sites, the equilibrium ratio
$N_{24d}/N_{8b} = 3\exp(\Delta E/\kT)$ is about 440 at 600~K, so the 8b site is practically empty at any processing
temperature of the devices. On 24d the W--O bonds are $1.892\times2$, $1.902\times2$ and $2.152\times2$~\AA{} (mean
1.982~\AA); on 8b six bonds of 1.972~\AA; in the films the mean is 2.000~\AA{} (1~nm) and 1.977~\AA{} (2~nm). These means
lie close to the 1.98~\AA{} expected from Shannon radii for W$^{6+}$ and well below the 2.04~\AA{} for W$^{4+}$
\citep{Shannon1976}, which is consistent with W$^{6+}$ by the ionic-radius argument \fact{041}; the bond lengths do not
measure a charge \corr{X11}.

Spin-polarised calculations of the bulk cells on the $3\times3\times3$ grid find weak moments (0.62 and 0.74~$\mu_B$ per
cell, 19.5 and 22.7~meV below the non-magnetic state). Coarse sampling of a degenerate electron gas can produce such
Stoner-like moments spuriously; they are not interpreted \fact{046} \corr{X7}.

\section{Where the W level lies and where the electrons go}
\label{sec:mono-w-level}

\paragraph{A corrected analysis.} On 30 September the W cells were analysed with the valence-band top taken as band
$n_{\mathrm{pure}}$. That placed a conduction-band state at the valence-band top and produced a ``W-5d level 0.19~eV
below the conduction band'' with $E_F$ pinned in it, which was read as a localised, deep donor in the 1~nm film, and an
unexplained shift of 1.1--1.65~eV of all levels on the vacuum scale. Both were artefacts of the band count
(\cref{sec:mono-dft-paw}). The analysis was corrected on 5 October and refined on 14 October with the wording below
\corr{X1}. The episode is kept in the record because the wrong reading led to a physical conclusion, a deep W donor,
that would have fitted the device data well and was nonetheless false.

\paragraph{Computed result.} In every cell (\cref{tab:mono-w-cells}) the state with the largest W-5d weight
(about 50--60\,\% at $\Gamma$) is a resonance inside the host conduction band, 1.2--1.8~eV above its bottom, and lies
within about 0.1~eV of $E_F$ at $\Gamma$; the host band below it is filled. In the 1~nm film the lowest conduction band
(band 312) lies below $E_F$ at every $k$ and holds two of the three donated electrons, and about one electron occupies the
W-5d-derived band. In the bulk 24d cell the lowest conduction band holds 1.79 and the next 1.13 electrons (1.93 and 0.95
for 8b). A Kane band with the computed bulk parameters can hold 2.0--2.2 electrons per cell up to the computed $E_F$
(a parabolic band only 0.75), consistent with this partition \fact{082}. The W-5d projected counts (0.09--0.43~e) are
smaller than the electrons in the W-rich band because that band is itself only about half W 5d.

\paragraph{Effect of W on the host bands.} W makes the host conduction band 5\,\% heavier in bulk \fact{047} and narrows
the gap between the valence-band top and the host conduction-band bottom by 0.069~eV (1~nm) and 0.148~eV (2~nm), while
the electron affinity of the host band rises by 0.14 and 0.25~eV \fact{048}. A narrowing of this size is consistent with
the band-gap renormalisation expected in degenerately doped \InO{} \citep{Hamberg1986} (\prov{inferred}); the cells do not
separate it from local strain and from the different valence-band character of the W films, whose valence-band top lies
mostly on surface OH oxygens (OH share 0.72), unlike the bulk-like top of the pure 2~nm film.

\paragraph{What is computed and what is inferred.} Computed, at the filling of the cells: the W-5d-rich level is
resonant 1.2--1.8~eV above the host band bottom and lies near $E_F$; the host band holds most of the donated electrons.
Interpreted: that the level is ``pinned'' at $E_F$ by the filling, which would need a series of cells with different
electron densities. Inferred, not computed: that W remains a fully ionised resonant donor at device densities, where
$E_F$ lies below the conduction-band edge; this needs a lower-density cell and a hybrid functional, because a hybrid
can lower localised d levels \claim{C022}. If the level stays at least 1.2~eV above $\Ec$, its occupation at device
densities is below $\exp(-1.2/0.0259)\approx10^{-20}$.

\section{Oxygen vacancies near and far from W (register H-L)}
\label{sec:mono-w-hl}

\paragraph{Registration.} H-L was added to the frozen register by \amend{A17} on 2026-10-10T18:48Z, before any vacancy
cell was built, and approved by the user (\dlog{D-19}). Six neutral 80-atom cells: In$_{32}$O$_{48}$ and
In$_{31}$WO$_{48}$ (references), In$_{32}$O$_{47}$ (vacancy in pure \InO{}), and In$_{31}$WO$_{47}$ with the vacancy
\emph{near} W (the O of the shortest W--O bond in the relaxed host) and \emph{far} from W (largest minimum-image distance,
O32, 6.85~\AA). Because the two shortest W--O pairs differed by only 0.0105~\AA, both near candidates (O30 and O72) were
computed, with the registered one chosen by the H-L-relaxed host (\amend{A19}). Settings: PBE, 71/568~Ry, $3\times3\times3$
relaxation, Gaussian 0.005~Ry, spin-unpolarised, fixed cell, forces $10^{-3}$~Ry/bohr, $4\times4\times4$ single points on
every relaxed geometry. The oxygen chemical potential cancels in both registered differences:
\begin{align}
\Delta E_{\mathrm{local}} &= E[\mathrm{In_{31}WO_{47}},\mathrm{near}] - E[\mathrm{In_{31}WO_{47}},\mathrm{far}]
 \;\ge\; +0.3~\mathrm{eV}, \label{eq:mono-w-elocal}\\
\Delta E_{\mathrm{dop}} &= \big(E[\mathrm{far}] - E[\mathrm{In_{31}WO_{48}}]\big) - \big(E[\mathrm{In_{32}O_{47}}] -
 E[\mathrm{In_{32}O_{48}}]\big) \;>\; +0.05~\mathrm{eV}. \label{eq:mono-w-edop}
\end{align}
Robustness rules (\amend{A20}): a change above 10~meV between the $k3$ and $k4$ grids means ``unresolved significance''; a
sensitivity (spin, smearing width 0.002~Ry) that shifts a difference by more than 50~meV or flips its verdict makes H-L
``not robust''. Forecasts recorded before any result: $+0.5$ to $+1.5$~eV for $\Delta E_{\mathrm{local}}$ and $+0.5$ to
$+3$~eV for $\Delta E_{\mathrm{dop}}$.

\paragraph{What the scores mean.} Both differences are positive and above their thresholds at every tested mesh and
width (\cref{tab:mono-w-hl}, \cref{fig:mono-w-hl}). $\Delta E_{\mathrm{dop}}$ changes by only 3.4~meV from $k3$ to $k4$ (which
does not by itself establish the convergence of the individual energies) and is robust to the width: in this degenerate crystalline cell, W raises the energy of a neutral oxygen vacancy far from it by 0.73~eV.
$\Delta E_{\mathrm{local}}$ changes by $-25.7$~meV from $k3$ to $k4$, more than the 10~meV rule allows, so its
significance is unresolved although both values exceed $+0.3$~eV; a vacancy next to W costs a further 0.53--0.56~eV.
The spin sensitivity was not run, so robustness is not established, and the overall register verdict is
\textsc{unresolved}; the record states explicitly that ``H-L supported'' must not be written. Both outcomes fall within the forecast ranges.

\paragraph{What the scores do not mean.} Neither difference is a formation energy at a device Fermi level
(\cref{sec:mono-defect-formation}). In the W cell the vacancy electrons are added to a conduction band already filled to
1.7~eV above its bottom, so $\Delta E_{\mathrm{dop}}$ mixes band filling with local chemistry, strain and image
interactions; at the device carrier densities the filling term would be much smaller, and its sign or size there is not
computed. The far site is only 6--8~\AA{} from W and its periodic images, so $\Delta E_{\mathrm{local}}$ is a cell-limited
estimate. No charged state, no finite-size correction and no hybrid functional enters, so no charge-transition level
follows \citep{Chatratin2019}. And the cells are crystalline. The sign of $\Delta E_{\mathrm{dop}}$ is the one that the
paper's statement that W suppresses oxygen vacancies would imply; with H-L unresolved overall and in a regime that does
not map onto the devices, the result neither supports nor tests that statement for the films.

\begin{keyresult}
In crystalline \InO{}, W substitutes on the 24d site (by 0.258~eV) with W--O bonds consistent with W$^{6+}$. At the
computed fillings ($1.4$--$3.0\times10^{21}$\cmcu{}), its W-5d-rich level is a resonance 1.2--1.8~eV above the host
conduction-band bottom, lying within about 0.1~eV of $E_F$, and the host band holds most of the donated electrons. An
earlier reading of a deep, localised W donor was a band-counting artefact and is withdrawn. That W stays a fully
ionised donor at device densities is inferred, not computed. In the registered vacancy test, W raises the neutral
vacancy energy by 0.73~eV far from W (robust within the tested settings) and by a further 0.53--0.56~eV next to it
(significance unresolved); H-L is unresolved overall, and its energies include band filling, so they are not formation
energies at device conditions.
\end{keyresult}
