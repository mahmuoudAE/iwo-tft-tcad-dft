\begin{frame}[t]{Upper transistor tiers must be processed below about 400\,\textdegree C}
  \oneTop
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.60\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}
        \item Geometric scaling has slowed since the end of Dennard scaling\sref{Theis2017}.
        \item Thinned channels lose performance: Si below about 3\,nm, InGaAs below about 10\,nm\sref[p.~164]{Si2022}.
        \item Monolithic 3-D integration stacks back-end-of-line (BEOL) transistor tiers above the Cu interconnect,
          which limits their processing to below about 400\,\textdegree C\sref[p.~1]{Januar2026}.
        \item This excludes crystalline Si; oxide semiconductors are the leading
          candidates\srefs{Datta2019,Salahuddin2018}. Their low off-current enables capacitor-less DRAM with
          retention beyond 400\,s\sref{Belmonte2020}.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}

\begin{frame}[t]{Indium oxide switches off only as a film a few nanometres thick}
  \oneTop
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
      {\small\textbf{Why oxide and \InO{} channels}\par}
      \begin{itemize}
        \item Amorphous oxides were developed for display transistors\srefs{Nomura2004,Kamiya2010,Fortunato2012}; their
          s-like conduction band survives disorder\sref{Medvedeva2017}.
        \item \InO{} switches off only when very thin: atomic-layer-deposited 0.7\,nm channels work in enhancement mode,
          with a threshold tuned by thickness and attributed to confinement\sref[p.~500]{Si2021}.
        \item Nanometre-thick \InO{}: drain currents above 10\,A/mm\sref[p.~21536]{Lin2022}.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.50\textwidth}
      {\small\textbf{What thinning costs}\par}
      \begin{itemize}
        \item Confinement opens the gap and moves the band edges (Part~6) \lab{computed}.
        \item Every atom is near a surface; conventional amorphous oxides become disorder-limited below about
          10\,nm\sref[p.~1]{Januar2026}.
        \item Device spread grows as donors become few: about 500 per \textmu m$^{2}$ in a 2.0\,nm channel at the
          calibrated effective density \num{2.5e17}\cmcu{} (not identifiable from $\Id$--$\Vg$) \lab{fitted}.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}\par}
  \vspace{1mm}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The device paper\footnotemark[5] (pp.~3, 4, 7) reports that W passivates oxygen vacancies, and relates the fall of the peak
      mobility from about 27.4 to 5.1\cmVs{} (13.2 to 2.0\,nm) to a roughly fourfold rise of the trap density.
    \item Apart from that paper's crystalline cells, the project's literature search found no peer-reviewed DFT study
      of W in \InO{}; those cells did not test the vacancy-passivation statement.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

        2.0  & 0.662 &  \\
        6.3  & 0.644 & \num{-0.018} \\
        13.2 & 0.083 & {\color{ntumaroon}\num{-0.561}} \\
        \bottomrule
      \end{tabular}\par}
      \vspace{0.5mm}\lab{derived}
      \src{Constant-current threshold at $\Id = 10^{-9}$\,A/\textmu m, $\Vd = 0.7$\,V (registered extraction);
        $\Delta$: change from the next thinner film; one device per film. A 31.8\,nm curve, supplied with the data but
        not part of the published 2.0--13.2\,nm series, never falls below $10^{-9}$\,A/\textmu m.
        Data: Prof.\ M.-H.\ Lee's group, NTU\oneTinyMark\sref{Januar2026}.}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.66\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}
        \item The constant-current threshold is 0.58\,V higher at 2.0 than at 13.2\,nm; 0.56\,V of it lies between
          13.2 and 6.3\,nm \lab{derived}.
        \item The paper fits eight parameters per device. This project's per-film models match each threshold and
          on-current by construction \lab{fitted}, yet miss the 6.3\,nm on-state shape and the 13.2\,nm swing
          \lab{computed}.
        \item All three out-of-sample predictions of the 6.3\,nm threshold failed ($-0.29$, $-0.37$, $-0.23$\,V)
          \lab{falsified}.
        \item A shared model needs material inputs instead, chiefly $\dEc(t)$ (affinity, hence $\Vth$), $m^{*}(t)$
          (density of states, band mobility) and whether W is ionised (its share of the carriers).
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \takeaway{The question: which of these inputs can first principles supply to one shared model?}
\end{frame}
